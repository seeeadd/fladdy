## In 5 lines
1. Open the transition with 3-4 "thus far" yes questions about the time just spent [doc L9081-9105].
2. Tell them, and mean it, that they can do it alone: that is choice one [doc L9117-9139].
3. Then: "But what if there were another option... to go at this together?" [doc L9151-9161].
4. Take on responsibility for their result, then ask "would you love to hear more?" before naming the program
   [doc L9173-9201, L9521-9527].
5. Prove it with specific numbers, names and places, answer objections before they come, end on one clear action
   [doc L9529-9547, L9613, L10135-10139].

## The rules

**Why a transition**
1. The notes graveyard: webinar notes end up in the attic or the garage and die there. Attending changes nothing;
   action does. The transition bridges learning and doing [doc L9035-9055].
2. Success needs the right tools, the right training and a turnkey solution; one webinar is valuable but not enough
   [doc L9057-9075].
3. The transition is a clear decision point where they choose the path that serves them: service, not selling
   [doc L9029, L10089-10091, L11107].

**The transition, in order**
4. Momentum recap: 3-4 easy yes questions. "Thus far" says value was given, "more confident" plants progress, "one
   or two things" sets a low bar, "excited to" adds emotion [doc L9081-9115].
5. DIY acknowledgment: with "absolute confidence", say they could do it alone. It removes pressure, shows respect,
   avoids push-back and sets up the contrast [doc L9117-9149, L10327-10337].
6. Pause, then the "what if" bridge: partnership ("together"), depth ("deeper than we can in one short hour"), "just
   this one time", and "what if" in place of "you should" [doc L9151-9171, L9493].
7. The obligation offer: you take "an obligation to your outcome". The seller now carries the risk; you sell
   responsibility for results, not features [doc L9173-9191, L10357-10371].
8. The empowerment offer: every resource "to stack the deck in your favor to make success all but inevitable"
   [doc L9193-9211].
9. The invitation, "would you love to hear more about it...?", then the program [doc L9521-9527].
10. Ask, don't tell: questions get agreement without resistance [doc L9213, L10093].

**What drives it**
11. Consistency: once they agree the hour was worth it, they want to keep going [doc L9217-9227].
12. Autonomy: the offer is an option, never a requirement [doc L9229-9245].
13. The gap: "knowing and DOING are different"; the program bridges them [doc L9247-9265].
14. Burden shift: "Your success becomes my responsibility" [doc L9267-9281].
15. Contrast: show the hard DIY path (trial and error, slow, uncertain) before yours (proven, fast, supported)
    [doc L9283-9311, L9413-9421].
16. Your confidence transfers to them [doc L10095]. Use inevitability words ("all but inevitable", "nearly
    impossible to fail") [doc L9325-9335] and future pacing ("Imagine if you had...") [doc L9437-9445].

**Introducing the program**
17. Order: name, promise, proof, components, investment (price and value stack), guarantee, bonuses, call to action
    [doc L9529-9547]; each part has its own playbook (offer, price, bonuses, risk). Show how each component solves a
    specific problem, never a bare feature list [doc L10117-10121].
18. Stack the proof: results, peers, an origin story, demonstration (real dashboards) [doc L9313-9323].
19. Specific numbers: "960 sellers" not "hundreds", "population 7261" not "small town" [doc L9787-9799,
    L10373-10395].
20. Origin story: humble start, struggle, breakthrough, duty to share, and a relatable trait in place of
    credentials ("stubbornness") [doc L9689-9733].
21. Borrowed authority (a third party that vouches), ongoing discovery (they keep finding new things) and community
    (people helping each other) [doc L9735-9785].
22. The confident promise: "Even if [no experience], I'm confident we can have you [result] in short order"
    [doc L9801-9819]. Raise the stakes: "the money that you deserve" [doc L9821-9827].

**Objections**
23. Handle them before they are raised [doc L9613-9615, L10101].
24. Excuse elimination: name it, show you understand, give counter-evidence, show how the program solves it
    [doc L9663-9687].
25. "No time": the program saves time [doc L9635-9643]. "Not sure how to make it work": "That's the biggie", and it
    is exactly what the program solves [doc L9645-9657]. "I can do it myself": the DIY acknowledgment already
    answers it [doc L9659-9661].

**Fit the audience**
26. Skeptical: point to results, not theory. Beginners: "That's exactly who this was designed for". Busy: "the RIGHT
    7 hours a week with the RIGHT guidance" [doc L10215-10253].
27. Up to $500: quick start, guarantee. $500-2,000: quick wins plus depth, community. $2,000+: transformation,
    partnership, ROI [doc L10255-10279].
28. Cold traffic: heavy proof, clear risk reversal. Warm: build on past contact. Past customers: "next level", VIP
    access [doc L10281-10305].

**The call to action**
29. One clear action ("Click the button below to secure your spot now", never "check out the link below"), what
    happens right after, the bonus for joining here, a legitimate reason to act now [doc L10135-10139,
    L10863-10877].
30. The close is the next logical step, not a pivot [doc L10107].
31. Track conversion, cart abandonment, common objections, drop-off and chat questions [doc L10071-10083].

## How to do it
The script, in order (his words, our brackets) [doc L9475-9527, L10739-10877]:
1. Recap: "Before we go any further, I want to check in with you about our time together so far. Thus far, has our
   time on this [format] been well spent? Do you now feel more confident about [topic]? Can you see how even just one
   or two things I showed you today can help you...? Are you excited to make a go out of this...?"
2. DIY: "I have absolute confidence that you could take everything we covered here today and on your own go out and
   put it into action and get results that you previously thought weren't possible. I am certain just with the
   information we've covered on this [format] today, left on your own with your own resources, time and effort you
   can make a go at this in a way most people will never be able to. And if that were your only option it would be a
   worthy ideal to pursue." [Pause.]
3. Bridge: "But what if there were another option. An option that allowed us to go at this together? A chance to go
   deeper than we can in one [short hour] on one [fast moving format] connecting together just this one time."
4. Responsibility: "What if I played an active role in your results, took a responsibility in your success and had
   an obligation to your outcome? How would that make things different?"
5. Empowerment: "Do you think in that situation I could empower you with any and all resources at my disposal to
   stack the deck in your favor to make success all but inevitable? If I had shortcuts and support systems and
   additional insights that go even deeper and more thorough into getting you the result you want, desire and quite
   frankly deserve. If I had such an option for you, would you love to hear more about it and what it could do for
   you?"
6. Program: "That's why it's with great pleasure that I introduce you to... [PROGRAM NAME]." Then what it is, who
   made it, "[number] students achieved [result]", the origin story, the community, the promise.
7. Call to action: "Simply click the [button] below to [action]. When you do, you'll [what happens]. Plus, as a bonus
   for joining through this [format], you'll also get [bonuses]. But you need to act now because [legitimate
   reason]."

Objection line: "Is it that [objection]?" / "I get it, [empathy]." / "But here's the thing: [counter-evidence]." /
"And in [program], [how it is solved]." [doc L10729-10737].

Test it: what did we cover, what can you do alone, what could we do together, why better, what proof, what next,
why now [doc L11025-11041]. The notes give it five minutes: recap; DIY and bridge; responsibility and empowerment;
program and proof; call to action [doc L11043-11047].

  mentoring, Special tools, Accountability, Resources, And more!", 5-42 his programs and student results, 43-48 the
  two choices close [doc L9829-9929].
- The Wholesale Formula: Amazon as authority; "960 sellers generated over $1.057 BILLION"; Dan and Dylan of Corbin,
  Kentucky turned a maxed $600 credit limit into $500,000 in sales in 6 months; the promise [doc L9549-9609].
- Product eClass, the call-out: "If we parted ways right now... would you immediately go out... and set the world on
  fire...?" "DOUBTFUL!" Old habits are the real obstacle, so "I won't let that happen!" [doc L10517-10591].
- "Excuses?": "Is it you don't like to sell? Too much competition? What about google?" [doc L10593-10613].

## Mistakes he calls out
- The hard pitch ("You need to buy this now!"), the value dump, the pressure close, weak proof ("Many people have
  succeeded"), an unclear call to action [doc L10109-10139].
- Dismissing concerns as "just excuses", false urgency, overpromising, running down competitors, making people feel
  stupid for not buying, being unclear about what they're buying [doc L11049-11063].
- Asking "How can I get them to buy?" instead of "How can I make the decision clear?" [doc L10895-10911].

**Jeremy Haynes, for comparison** (from his teardown; not adopted until Sean says yes)
- Bridge: Jeremy (Russell Brunson's "ask to pitch") asks in the chat, "Just give me a simple yes. Can I make you an
  offer?", and goes in when it floods with yes [Jeremy 1:14:54-1:15:46]. Jason's is a monologue ending "would you
  love to hear more?" [doc L9475-9523]. Both concede they will be better off even alone [Jeremy 1:15:13].
- Stack: Jeremy goes item by item, their problem or wanted outcome, the item, "so you can" get the outcome, then one
  whole-stack slide [Jeremy 1:16:23-1:18:36]. Jason leads with name, promise and proof [doc L9531-9547].
- Price: Jeremy may leave it out (more, less qualified calls) or say it (fewer, more qualified); he says it
  [Jeremy 1:19:03]. Jason has an investment step and "transparent pricing" [doc L9541, L11077].
- Loop: Jeremy cycles objections, risk reversal, Q&A, repitch until risk reversals run out, then objections, Q&A,
  repitch, then an outro [Jeremy 1:19:42-1:23:04]. Jason answers objections ahead of time, no Q&A or repitch
  [doc L9613-9661].
- Jeremy splits objections (reasons not to buy) from risk reversals (taking the buyer's risk, a pay-per-lead agency
  vs a retainer) [Jeremy 1:21:20]; Jason's notes have one guarantee step [doc L9543].
- Jeremy's close runs an hour or more and sells a booked call [Jeremy 1:14:08, 1:23:33]; Jason's, in the notes, is
  five minutes to a buy button [doc L11043-11047, L10877].
- Jeremy asks for "ones in the chat" during the stack too [Jeremy 1:18:36]; Jason's close notes ask for none.
