# Close playbook

Read this when: writing the transition into the pitch and the close of a webinar, a VSL or a video CTA.

(`jeremy-haynes-videos/01 The Most Valuable Webinar Training Youll Ever Watch (P1v-AN3So3A)/teardown.md`)

## In 5 lines
1. Open the transition with 3-4 "thus far" yes questions about the time just spent [doc L9081-9105].
2. Tell them, and mean it, that they can do it alone: that is choice one [doc L9117-9139].
3. Then: "But what if there were another option... to go at this together?" [doc L9151-9161].
4. Take on responsibility for their result, then ask "would you love to hear more?" before you name the program
   [doc L9173-9201, L9521-9527].
5. Name the program, prove it with specific numbers, names and places, answer objections before they come, and end
   on one clear action [doc L9529-9547, L9613, L10135-10139].

## The rules

**Why a transition**
1. The notes graveyard: webinar notes end up "at home, at the office, in the attic, the garage..." and die there.
   Attending changes nothing; action does. The transition bridges learning and doing [doc L9035-9055].
2. Success needs the right tools, the right training and a turnkey (done-for-you) solution. One webinar is valuable
   but not enough: "You need more than that" [doc L9057-9075].
3. The transition is a clear decision point where they choose the path that serves them; it is service, not selling
   [doc L9029, L10089-10091, L11107].

**The transition, in order**
4. Momentum recap: 3-4 easy yes questions. "Thus far" says value is already given, "more confident" plants progress,
   "one or two things" sets a low bar, "excited to" adds emotion [doc L9081-9115].
5. DIY acknowledgment: say, with "absolute confidence", that they could do it alone and get results they thought
   impossible, and that it would be "a worthy ideal to pursue". It removes pressure, shows respect, avoids push-back
   and sets up the contrast [doc L9117-9149, L10327-10337].
6. Pause, then the "what if" bridge: partnership ("together"), depth ("deeper than we can in one short hour"), "just
   this one time", and "what if" in place of "you should" [doc L9151-9171, L9493].
7. The obligation offer: you take an active role, a responsibility and "an obligation to your outcome". The seller
   now carries the risk; you sell responsibility for results, not features [doc L9173-9191, L10357-10371].
8. The empowerment offer: "any and all resources at my disposal to stack the deck in your favor to make success all
   but inevitable" [doc L9193-9211].
9. The invitation: "would you love to hear more about it", then "That's why it's with great pleasure that I
   introduce you to... [PROGRAM NAME]" [doc L9521-9527].
10. Ask, don't tell: questions get agreement without resistance [doc L9213, L10093].

**What drives it**
11. Consistency: once they agree the hour was worth it, they want to keep going ("imagine what we can accomplish
    together over the next 7 weeks") [doc L9217-9227].
12. Autonomy: always say they can do it alone; the offer is an option, never a requirement [doc L9229-9245, L10089].
13. The gap: the webinar shows what is possible, the transition shows that "knowing and DOING are different", the
    program bridges it [doc L9247-9265].
14. Burden shift: from "I need to convince you to buy" to "Your success becomes my responsibility"
    [doc L9267-9281, L10103].
15. Contrast: always show the hard DIY path (trial and error, slow, uncertain) before your path (step by step,
    proven, fast, supported) [doc L9283-9311, L9413-9421].
16. Confidence transfers: "I'm confident you can" becomes "I'm confident I can" [doc L10095].
17. Inevitability words: "all but inevitable", "stack the deck in your favor", "nearly impossible to fail"
    [doc L9325-9335].
18. Future pace: "Imagine if you had a complete system, proven methods, and ongoing support. How would that change
    your timeline to success?" [doc L9437-9445].

**Introducing the program**
19. Order: name, promise, proof, components, investment (price and value stack), guarantee, bonuses, call to action
    [doc L9529-9547]. Show how each component solves a specific problem; never a bare list of features
    [doc L10117-10121].
20. Stack the proof: results, peers, an origin story, demonstration (real dashboards) [doc L9313-9323,
    L10025-10037].
21. Specific numbers: "960 sellers" not "hundreds", "$1.057 BILLION" not "over a billion", "population 7261" not
    "small town" [doc L9787-9799, L10373-10395].
22. Origin story: humble start, struggle, breakthrough, duty to share, plus a relatable trait in place of
    credentials ("stubbornness") [doc L9689-9733, L10445-10469].
23. Borrowed authority: a third party that vouches ("exactly what Amazon wants its sellers to follow")
    [doc L9735-9747].
24. Ongoing discovery: they keep finding new things, so there is more to come [doc L9749-9763].
25. Community proof: people who help each other prove it works and make them want to belong
    [doc L9765-9785, L10105].
26. The confident promise: "Even if [no experience], I'm confident we can have you [result] in short order through
    [program]" [doc L9801-9819].
27. Raise the stakes: "which are not making you the money that you deserve!" turns nice to have into deserve to have
    [doc L9821-9827].

**Objections**
28. Handle them before they are raised [doc L9613-9615, L10101].
29. Excuse elimination: name it, show you understand, give counter-evidence, show how the program solves it
    [doc L9663-9687].
30. "I don't have time": position the program as saving time, not taking it [doc L9635-9643].
31. "Not sure how to make it work": call it "the biggie", the biggest objection, and say it is exactly what the
    program solves [doc L9645-9657].
32. "I can figure it out myself" is already answered by the DIY acknowledgment [doc L9659-9661].

**Fit the audience**
33. Skeptical market: welcome the skepticism and point to results, not theory. Beginners: "even if you're starting
    from absolute zero... That's exactly who this was designed for". Busy people: "You don't need 40 hours a week.
    You need the RIGHT 7 hours a week with the RIGHT guidance" [doc L10215-10253].
34. Price: up to $500, quick start, immediate value, guarantee; $500-2,000, quick wins plus depth, support and
    community; $2,000+, transformation, partnership and access, ROI [doc L10255-10279].
35. Relationship: cold traffic gets heavy proof, clear risk reversal and several proof types; warm builds on past
    contact; past customers get "next level" and VIP or priority access [doc L10281-10305].

**The call to action**
36. One clear action: "Click the button below to secure your spot now", never "If you're interested, check out the
    link below" [doc L10135-10139]. Say what happens right after, the bonus for joining here and a legitimate reason
    to act now [doc L10863-10877].
37. The close is the next logical step, not a pivot [doc L10107].
38. Track conversion, cart abandonment, the most common objections, where people drop off and chat questions
    [doc L10071-10083].

## How to do it
The full script, in order (his words, our brackets) [doc L9475-9527, L10739-10877]:
1. Recap: "Before we go any further, I want to check in with you about our time together so far. Thus far, has our
   time on this [format] been well spent? Do you now feel more confident about [topic]? Can you see how even just one
   or two things I showed you today can help you in your [area], regardless of whether you use it for [main use] or
   somewhere else? Are you excited to make a go out of this to see what wonderful things it opens up for you in
   [area / life]?"
2. DIY: "Now here's what I know about you... I have absolute confidence that you could take everything we covered
   here today and on your own go out and put it into action and get results that you previously thought weren't
   possible. I am certain just with the information we've covered on this [format] today, left on your own with your
   own resources, time and effort you can make a go at this in a way most people will never be able to. You're
   smart. You're capable. You're resourceful. And if that were your only option it would be a worthy ideal to
   pursue." [Pause.]
3. Bridge: "But what if there were another option. An option that allowed us to go at this together? A chance to go
   deeper than we can in one [short hour] on one [fast moving format] connecting together just this one time."
4. Responsibility: "What if I played an active role in your results? What if I took a responsibility in your
   success? What if I had an obligation to your outcome? How would that make things different?"
5. Empowerment and invitation: "Do you think in that situation I could empower you with any and all resources at my
   disposal to stack the deck in your favor to make success all but inevitable? If I had shortcuts and support
   systems and additional insights that go even deeper and more thorough into getting you the result you want,
   desire and quite frankly deserve... If I had such an option for you, would you love to hear more about it and
   what it could do for you?"
6. Program: "That's why it's with great pleasure that I introduce you to... [PROGRAM NAME]." Then what it is; who
   made it and why they are credible; "[number] students achieve[d] [result] including [amount]"; the origin story;
   what they keep discovering; the community of [number]; the promise: "Even if you're starting from zero... I'm
   confident we can have you achieving [result] in [time] through [program]."
7. Call to action: "Simply click the [button] below to [action]. When you do, you'll [what happens immediately].
   Plus, as a bonus for joining through this [format], you'll also get [bonuses]. But you need to act now because
   [legitimate scarcity reason]. Click the button below to get started now."

Objection line: "Is it that [objection]?" / "I get it, [empathy]." / "But here's the thing: [counter-evidence]." /
"And in [program], [how it is solved]." [doc L10729-10737].

Before writing, gather: number of customers, their result, the origin story, the trait that made it possible, why
they share, community size, recent discoveries [doc L10673-10689].

Check it with the 7-second test: what did we cover; what can you do alone; what could we do together; why is that
better; what's the proof; what's the next step; why now [doc L11025-11041]. The notes time it at five minutes: recap,
DIY and bridge, responsibility and empowerment, program and proof, call to action, one minute each [doc L11043-11047].

## Mistakes he calls out
- The hard pitch: "You need to buy this now!" in place of "Would you love to hear about an option?"
  [doc L10111-10115].
- The value dump: features with no context [doc L10117-10121].
- The pressure close: making them feel forced [doc L10123-10127].
- Weak proof: "Many people have succeeded" [doc L10129-10133].
- An unclear call to action [doc L10135-10139].
- Red flags: dismissing concerns as "just excuses", false urgency, overpromising, running down competitors, making
  people feel stupid for not buying, high pressure, being unclear about what they're buying [doc L11049-11063].
- The wrong questions: "How can I get them to buy?", "What tricks will increase conversion?". Ask instead "How can I
  make the decision clear?", "How do I remove genuine barriers?" [doc L10895-10911].
