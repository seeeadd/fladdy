# Price playbook

Read this when: revealing or justifying a price, price anchors, payment plans, value vs price (a webinar's price
reveal, a client's Day 3 close, a checkout or sales page).
Sources: `sources/fladlien/notes/full-methodology.md` L12507-14562 (Offer lessons, Jason's step 11: price); cross-checks
in the Jason slides and Jeremy teardowns. Built 2026-10-29. The source is the master: re-check a quote there.

## In 5 lines
1. Whatever the price, it must feel like a deal: "NO MATTER THE PRICE, IT'S ALWAYS A DEAL!"
2. Build value for 10-15 minutes and preframe why this deal exists before saying any number.
3. Anchor from high to low: what DIY costs them, what it cost you, what others charge, what it "should" cost, the normal price, today's price.
4. Give a reason why, real scarcity in layers, and options: tiers, or one payment vs a plan.
5. Bonuses come after the price; then scarcity, urgency, one call to action, and what happens after they buy.

## The rules

**The 7 elements** [doc L12517-12873]
1. Preframe: before any number, explain the business reason for the price (market share, partnership, beta, limited capacity). Make clear it is not normal pricing and will not last; an opportunity, never desperation. It lowers resistance before the price is said. [doc L12519-12551]
2. DIY vs invest: price every part they would need to build it themselves, with their time (hourly rate × time), tools, the learning curve and mistakes, until DIY looks overwhelming: 5-20x your price. DIY cost = (hourly rate × time) + tools + resources + learning curve + mistakes. [doc L12553-12595]
3. Personal cost: what you invested to create it. Cross out rising numbers to build tension and land on a specific, believable, not round number; a "+" says the spending goes on. [doc L12597-12645]
4. Actual retail prices: what 3-5 comparable offers really charge, known names when possible, and what they leave out that you include. [doc L12653-12681]
5. Scarcity must be real and proven (chat logs, timestamps, past sellouts). Types: time, quantity, qualification (webinar attendees only), history. Several layers beat one. [doc L12683-12741]
6. Reason why: personal and emotional ("Because you've taken the time to be on this webinar today..."). Acknowledge their time and action, make them feel chosen, and add a strategic business reason. [doc L12743-12787]
7. Juxtaposition: 3 tiers minimum. The middle is the target; the top makes it look reasonable; the bottom makes the others look valuable (decoy, anchoring, compromise). Savings highlighted (yellow = savings, green = best value). Middle about 3-4x the base, top 5-10x. [doc L12789-12873]

**Anchoring**
8. Every number before your price is an anchor. Go from high to low; each one resets expectations. [doc L12893-12901]
9. Ask "What would be a fair investment for something like this?", let them think of a number, then reveal. [doc L12887-12891]
10. Show at least 3-4 higher price points before the reveal. [doc L14151-14155]
11. Work out your ratios: the price as a share of the DIY cost, your investment and a competitor's price ($497 is 1/15, 1/320 and 1/20). [doc L13681-13693]
12. Stack authority before the price: your results, your students' results, recognition, a comparison ("What [celebrity] is to [field]..."), what you spent building it. [doc L13763-13777]

**The reveal**
13. Reveal in stages, 20-30 minutes in all: value, DIY, your investment, market prices, a high anchor, the normal price, the special price, payment options, bonuses, scarcity. [doc L13869-13893]
14. Offer one payment or a plan. One payment: lower total, the guarantee added, for all-in buyers. Plan: a slightly higher total, easier to say yes to; more people buy. [doc L13409-13433, L13809-13837]
15. Continuity (a public price plus a monthly fee) makes dropping the fee a strong founding-member incentive. [doc L13435-13447]
16. Keep bonuses out of the price reveal and bring them after it: "And we haven't even discussed the bonuses yet!" [doc L13839-13865]
17. Qualify: "This isn't for everyone", who it is for, "If this seems too much, don't buy it". Self-selection cuts refunds. [doc L13895-13915]
18. "Wrong person" pricing: an incredible deal for the right person, fantastic for the kind-of-right, beautiful for the wrong one; then reveal less. That lowest price becomes the floor. [doc L13917-13937]
19. Future pace: what happens once they click (access now, within 24 hours, next week, in 30 days). [doc L13939-13961]
20. The takeaway: what they lose after [date]: a higher price, no bonuses, no payment plan. [doc L13963-13989]
21. Pre-handle objections. "Too expensive": DIY, market prices, your investment, "don't buy it". "I need to think": scarcity. "What if it doesn't work": guarantee and proof. "No time": "This saves you 8 weeks of work". [doc L13779-13807]
22. Give a "because" for the deal; any reason raises compliance. Write 5. [doc L13745-13761]
23. Scarcity stack: at least 3 real layers from time, quantity, qualifier, bonuses that expire before the offer, a coming price rise. [doc L13731-13743]
24. To existing customers: say they don't need it and lose nothing by passing, "Only invest if you feel you'll get more than [amount] out of this", set delivery dates, sales final. [doc L13341-13407]
25. Stack several triggers, never one: anchoring, loss aversion ("This price goes away..."), social proof (live chat logs of buyers, sold-out launches), authority, reciprocity, contrast. [doc L13573-13677, L14367]
26. Never apologise for your price. [doc L14405]

**Measuring**
27. Track show rate, stay rate to the price, conversion, cart value, tier split and payment split. His targets: show 40-50%+, stay 60-70%+, conversion 5-10%+ cold and 15-30%+ warm, 70-80% on the plan. Test anchors, tier names, multiples, bonuses, scarcity and deadline lengths. [doc L14317-14355]

## How to do it
The script [doc L13449-13571]:
```
1  Build value: the problem, the solution, proof, authority. No price yet.
2  Preframe: "So let's talk about investment... but before I do, I want to give you some context..." [strategic reason]
3  DIY: "If you wanted to create this yourself, here's what it would cost you: [part]: $X ... Your time: $X. Total DIY Cost: $X"
4  Your investment: "What do you think I've invested to create this?" [cross out numbers] "I've personally invested $X"
5  Market: "Similar solutions in the market charge: [A]: $X, [B]: $X, industry standard: $X"
6  Anchor: "So what would be fair for something like this? $X would be reasonable... The 'gurus' would charge $X"
7  Scarcity: "Now, we can't offer this to everyone... only available to [qualifier] and only until [deadline]"
8  Reason why: "But here's why I'm making this available to you: [personal] [strategic]. Because you [action], I want to..."
9  Reveal: "So normally this would be $X... But for [reason], we're offering it for: [tiers] or [$X one-time OR $X x 3 payments]"
10 Objections: "Now, I know what some of you might be thinking... [objection] But remember [value recap]"
11 Urgency and CTA: "This offer expires [when]. Only [X] spots available. Bonuses expire [when]. Click the button below to [action]"
```

The reveal card [doc L14533-14547]: "You can get started today for: $[___] one-time (best value + guarantee) or $[___] x
[___] payments (most accessible). Plus these bonuses valued at $[___]. Total value: $[___]. Your investment: $[___].
Savings: $[___]."

## Mistakes he calls out
The ten [doc L14141-14203]: price too early (10-15 minutes of value first); too few anchors (3-4 before the reveal);
"limited time" with no specifics; no reason why (give a strategic and an emotional one); one price point; no payment
plan; bonuses inside the first value build; nothing to compare; fake scarcity ("destroys trust"); no future pace.
Also: apologising for the price [doc L14405].

## After Harvest
From the doc:
- Preframe with market entry, partnership, beta or limited capacity; frame the deal as them helping us reach a bigger goal (market share, testimonials, case studies). [doc L12543-12551]
- DIY: every deliverable at market rate, plus opportunity cost and the "trial and error" tax. Personal cost: our years, courses, failed tests, tools, coaching, travel, books. [doc L12581-12591, L12625-12645]
- Real scarcity sources: onboarding capacity, founding or launch pricing, partnership limits, our time, platform limits. [doc L12717-12729]
- Tiers: DIY ($X), done-with-you ($3X, the target), done-for-you ($5-10X). [doc L12829-12873]
- A 4-week plan: numbers, tiers and bonuses; preframe, anchors and scarcity; scripts, visuals and a timed run-through; practice, order pages, launch, track. [doc L14205-14315]

Our reading:
- Ours: every anchor is a real number we can show, never an invented cost, price or spot. Jason turns real receipts from the story into the later price anchor [Jason slides 8:16-8:37].
- Ours: on the slide the price is not giant ("$10,000 in a giant font seems more expensive than $10,000 in a smaller font") and the total value is bigger than the investment [Jason slides 41:22, 41:47].
- Ours: the anchor sequences mix numbers from different launches ($7,900 Affiliorama, $159,134.92 One Stop Shop) [doc L12879-12917]: a pattern, not one real pitch.
- Ours: our own videos offer a free event plan, so these steps serve client webinar decks and Day 3 closes.
