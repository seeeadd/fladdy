# Offer playbook

Read this when: building or presenting an offer, the stack, what is included.
Sources: `sources/fladlien/notes/full-methodology.md` L11151-12506 (Offer lessons, Jason's step 10); cross-checks in
the Jason slides, Jason new webinar and Jeremy teardowns. Built 2026-10-29. The source is the master: re-check a quote there.

## In 5 lines
1. The offer slide is the most important slide: core plus every bonus, value stacked, one call to action.
2. Score every part on the 8 value criteria; under 6 of 8, fix it or cut it.
3. 3 to 7 parts. The core sells alone; the bonuses make it a no-brainer.
4. Name it, number it, show it, make it specific, call the price "your investment".
5. End with total value, your investment, a guarantee, one URL and a real deadline.

## The rules

**The offer slide**
1. The offer slide is the most important slide: everything converges there. It is a slide, never only spoken; it shows the core and all bonuses, stacks the value until the total is undeniable, and has a clear call to action with the link. [doc L11159-11171]
2. The presentation earns the offer: desire, urgency, authority and value first. Content, then desire, then offer; never offer, then hope they buy. [doc L11969-11989]
3. A finished offer slide gives clarity, value (worth more than you pay), proof, no risk and urgency. [doc L12489-12499]

**Choosing the parts**
4. Brainstorm everything you could offer before filtering: training, tools, templates, community, access, coaching, done-for-you. [doc L11177-11183]
5. Give the most value to parts that meet the 8 criteria [doc L11195-11257]:
   - Easy to explain: one sentence, or it is too complex. Simplicity is perceived value.
   - Demonstrable: you can show it working. Screenshots beat descriptions.
   - High retail value: what someone else charges, from real market prices.
   - High perceived value: what it feels worth (time saved, problems solved, access gained).
   - Novel: "Exclusive," "Never-before-seen," "Only available here."
   - Objection crushing: removes a reason not to buy or a risk, makes success more certain.
   - Cheap to deliver in time and money, so you can afford it. Digital beats physical.
   - Scales: recorded content scales infinitely; live coaching does not, but adds value.
6. Tick each part against the 8. Fewer than 6 ticks: reconsider it. [doc L12085-12105]
7. 1-2 parts feel incomplete ("That's it?"); 10+ overwhelm and lower each part's value. 3-7 parts fill one slide that reads at a glance. [doc L11259-11285]
8. The core is what they buy: the main transformational promise you must deliver. Bonuses are accelerators and insurance: easier, fewer obstacles, more certainty. The core must be strong enough to sell alone. [doc L11299-11319]

**Shaping the parts**
9. Package access as a named product. The name is one word if possible, suggests the transformation and is easy to repeat. [doc L11439, L11995-12001]
10. Everything is named ("Better Than Money Back Promise"), numbered ("$13,082.45 total value"), visual (screenshots over text, diagrams over paragraphs, before/after), specific ("30-day guaranteed response time to every question") and called an investment, never a cost. [doc L12349-12391]
11. Give the offer a story: why it exists, who made it, what qualifies them, a personal connection. [doc L12003-12011]
12. Number and title the sessions or modules ("7 Sessions covering...", then Session 1, 2, 3). [doc L12107-12121]
13. Each part is concrete ("6 recorded workshops you can watch today"), live parts have dates and times, and each gets its what, why and how. [doc L12293-12299, L11729, L11759]
14. Build proof in: named people with specific results, photos, numbers, screenshots. [doc L12129-12139]
15. Show the work and cost that went into a part (years, hard cost, ad dollars). [doc L11619-11627, L11659]
16. Add done-with-you help where you can: review their first work, help close their first deal. [doc L11557, L12141-12147]
17. Every part should raise the dream outcome and its likelihood and cut the time delay and effort: Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) / (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice). [doc L12233-12245]
18. Simplify each revision: fewer words, show instead of say, remove or merge any part whose loss costs no impact. Jason went from 6 mechanisms to 5 to 4 to 3. [doc L12267-12281]
19. You sell a transformation: believable (proof), achievable (broken down), fast, certain (risk removed), valuable (high ROI). [doc L12393-12409]

**The order of the reveal** [doc L11341-11385]
20. Ascending: smallest bonus first, build to the core, end on the highest-value bonus (a crescendo).
21. Descending: lead with the core, then bonuses that each remove an objection ("and there's more").
22. Problem-solution pairs: a problem, the part that solves it, the next problem. This builds logical necessity.

**Price, guarantee, call to action**
23. Anchor the value ("This would normally cost...", "Compared to [competitor] at $X..."), then a simple price: one or two options at most, a payment plan if it fits, an urgency element. Details: the price playbook. [doc L12023-12037]
24. The guarantee removes all risk, beats money back when it can, and states clear requirements. The ladder: 0 none, 1 money back, 2 60-90 days, 3 better than money back, 4 we do it for you, 5 we refund you and pay you. Aim for 3 or higher. [doc L12039-12045, L12257-12265]
25. The call to action: one specific URL, what happens next, a time limit. [doc L12047-12053]
26. Scarcity comes from real limits (70 seats because of the room). [doc L11495]
27. The test: "This is expensive" means an offer problem; "This is cheap", you may have won; "This is a no-brainer", you won. [doc L12247-12255]

**Special cases**
28. Selling someone else's product: exclusive bonuses only for buying through your link. [doc L11489-11499, L12157-12167]
29. A special deal needs its reason: normally $X, but because of [beta, partnership, leverage], for you [price]. [doc L12149-12155, L11805]
30. High-ticket or service offers: a scheduler link, "30 minutes or less", a benefit preview. For skeptics, "I can't promise" they become a client: a consultation, permission to say no. [doc L11879-11935]
31. Lead magnet first: thank them, a free book with a specific promise and proof, then the consultation. [doc L11937-11967]

## How to do it
1. Brainstorm, score, split core from bonuses, name and number every part, value each bonus.
2. Pick a reveal order.
3. Assemble the 7 components: name, story, the stack, value anchoring, your price, guarantee, call to action. [doc L11991-12053]
4. Build the slide, then audit: named? stacked visually? numbered and specific? proof built in? [doc L12415-12423]

The offer slide [doc L12301-12345]:
```
[OFFER NAME as the headline]
THE [CORE THING]: what it is (1 line) / what's included (3-5 bullets) / what you'll achieve
BONUS #1: [NAME] (Value: $[X,XXX]): what it is / why it matters
BONUS #2 and #3: the same
TOTAL VALUE: $[XX,XXX]
YOUR INVESTMENT: $[X,XXX]   (or: performance-based, [X]% of results)
GUARANTEE: [your specific risk reversal]
CLICK HERE TO [ACTION]: [URL]
[Expires [date]] or [Only [X] spots]
```

The reason for a deal [doc L12149-12155]: "Normally this would cost $[X] if you [hired an agency]. But because [reason], for
you, it's just [price] + [incentive]."

## Mistakes he calls out
- The offer only spoken. [doc L11165]
- Unnamed parts: "Access to training", "community", "guarantee". [doc L11439, L12351-12357]
- Vague: "several sessions", "We've helped many clients", generic testimonials. [doc L12131, L12169-12177, L12359-12365]
- Parts you cannot show: "Access to our knowledge", "Coaching and support", "Expert guidance". [doc L12285-12291]
- Offer first, then hoping they buy. [doc L11987-11989]
- An offer slide that is unclear, weak or overwhelming. [doc L12479-12487]
