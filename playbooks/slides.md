# Slides playbook

Read this when: designing any slide, deck, YouTube visual or VSL visual (webinar decks, Day 1-3 event decks, the
YouTube slides, the VSL).
Sources: `jason-fladlien-videos/01 How to Design Webinar Slides Nobody Does This Right (rAwgv2hThVw)/teardown.md`
(main source, whole; [Jason slides m:ss]); `rules/slide-method.md` ([slide-method §n]); the "What is on screen"
sections of the teardowns in `jason-fladlien-videos/02`, `03`, `04` ([Jason new webinar], [Jason new way], [Jason AI])
and `jeremy-haynes-videos/01` ([Jeremy]); `rulings.md`. Built 2026-10-29. The source is the master: re-check a quote
there.

## In 5 lines

1. Ask "How do we show this?" of every line; answer with a real thing: a screenshot, a receipt, a real photo, an
   object with real printed detail [Jason slides 37:09, 20:05; slide-method §1].
2. The most important words are the biggest; titles, names, numbers and prices go small [Jason slides 1:55].
3. Slides are free: one idea per slide, each item appearing as it is said [Jason slides 6:09, 21:18].
4. Two colours; cut everything that does not persuade [Jason slides 7:06, 5:34].
5. Sean's rulings govern the look today: nicely made, one focal object, a different layout each slide, no template
   tells [ruling 2026-10-28].

## The rules

**Start from the line**
1. Start from the script's exact line, never a layout: write the belief the slide must leave, how to show it, and the
   build (the order things appear as said); then pick the layout [slide-method §1].
2. Ask "How do we show this?" of every slide. Show, don't tell: "I didn't say you post. I showed the post from
   LinkedIn" [Jason slides 37:09, 2:08, 17:35].
3. Real proof first: the photo from that year, the real receipt (a later price anchor). Stock only when nothing real
   exists; AI only to stand in for a missing real photo or animate a real document [Jason slides 7:22-9:07, 10:07].
   (Q2)
4. Screenshot the reality: do the real search, screenshot the pieces, strip the noise, combine them, keep the source
   line under it, highlight the key line only [Jason slides 19:36-20:50; Jason AI 1:19].
5. Show a number as countable things (30 houses, 350 clocks, 100 faces, a calendar), or alter the real document (the
   8 of 820 crossed out) [Jason slides 9:39-10:15, 35:42, 37:54].
6. Carry a metaphor consistently or drop it; never "levels", a "ladder" or a "wheel" the picture does not match
   [Jason slides 16:20-16:52, 24:19, 32:03, 40:22].

**Hierarchy and type**
7. The biggest words are the most important. Usually least important: the title, the presenter's name, the system's
   name, numbers, step numbers, the price [Jason slides 1:42-1:59, 11:31, 13:18, 23:42, 27:40, 41:22].
8. Titles tell the story in active voice; a label becomes the question the slide answers [Jason slides 22:52, 26:05].
9. Lists read top to bottom. Side by side only for a true sequence; no sideways text [Jason slides 1:26, 32:12-32:52].
10. Two colours: ink plus one accent; the key piece bolder, bigger, in the accent [Jason slides 7:06, 10:44].
    Colours may separate the steps of a process [Jason slides 33:37].
11. Inside one sentence, the less important half goes smaller, same font [Jason slides 7:55-8:06].
12. One font, three faces (bold, semibold, normal); he tends not to use italics [Jason slides 13:35, 33:10]. Fladdy's
    type today: Olivera headlines, Literata subheads, Hanken Grotesk body, Plex Mono labels; the Olivera demo has no
    punctuation, so headlines work around it [ruling 2026-10-28; slide-method "Type"]. (Q3)
13. Bigger fonts beat looks: the best buyers are "30, 40, 50 plus" [Jason slides 14:02-14:33].
14. No abbreviations they may not know [Jason slides 40:45]. No numbering when they can count to three; step numbers
    tiny; one word per step [Jason slides 28:10, 33:32].

**Cut what does not persuade**
15. Remove decorative swooshes, logos, stock photos, taglines (title slide at most), the presenter's name and photo
    (the webcam shows them), highlighter graphics, icons, glows, background images, level labels [Jason slides
    5:34-5:56, 11:20-11:58, 13:22, 22:37-22:45, 23:31, 24:08].
16. Noise only as overwhelming proof, such as a wall of before-and-after photos [Jason slides 27:47-28:06].
17. Functional beats cool; pretty costs 10-30 more minutes a slide and must earn it [Jason slides 12:45, 36:47-37:06].
    Too professional reads less trustworthy: "better to look ugly" [Jason slides 15:46-16:12]. (Q1)
18. Elaborate designs trap you; step slides reuse the mechanism slide's own element [Jason slides 33:47-34:25].
19. Type over a photo: nothing key behind the words; a black box at lowered opacity over it [Jason slides 39:22-40:08].

**Many slides, built in order**
20. Split rather than crowd, unless splitting breaks the context ("Two words, two dollar figures, one slide") [Jason
    slides 6:05-6:51, 28:57-29:28].
21. His webinars run about 500 slides, with motion every six seconds [Jason slides 28:44-28:52].
22. Animate to focus attention: one item at a time, in reading order, as said; a list that must stay up gets a moving
    highlight instead [Jason slides 9:25, 12:10-12:14, 21:18].
23. Storyboard: reuse the same pictures across slides so they tell one story; "You can do so much with arrows and
    screenshots" [Jason slides 18:42-18:54, 21:54].
24. Running maths goes in a table built row by row, then the dramatic reveal over it [Jason slides 29:55-31:48].
25. Compare in threes: "two is a coincidence, but three is a pattern" [Jason slides 38:45-38:53].
26. Emojis as type: copy, paste, size like a letter (24-72 points), "because they're functional" [Jason slides
    37:54-38:24]. (Q4)

**Slides that come back**
27. Each key slide is seen three times: intro setup, teaching, recap ("so far today we've discussed") [Jason slides
    3:37-3:58].
28. Tease the deck in the intro with a slide inside a slide; end on a wall of slides cut off at the bottom [Jason
    slides 2:48-4:38].
29. The mechanism slide is the most important content slide: name small and off-centre, small arrows, no "level"
    labels, a tagline per step, easy to change live, revisited with progress [Jason slides 23:04-25:00, 27:28].
30. Every deck has his call to action slide, "My favorite slide ... Everybody should have it" (template below) [Jason
    slides 40:58-42:14].

**Nicely made: Sean's rulings (these govern today)**
31. One focal photo object per slide, shot like a product photo (generated, cut out with rembg, a soft two-layer
    shadow); wide margins (112px); nothing collides; the headline has its own space, hand-broken [ruling 2026-10-28;
    slide-method §3].
32. One precise hand mark at most (the website underline or `ink.js`); no handwriting font (Virgil notes only on the
    Figma whiteboards); a slight tilt reads human, disorder reads unfinished; a circle never crosses its letters
    [ruling 2026-10-28; slide-method §3, §4].
33. The object carries real printed detail ("Admit one · live online", "No. 0001") and repeats (the tickets return as
    the Day 1-3 agenda); a blank box reads as a placeholder [slide-method §3b].
34. YouTube slides stay simple and adaptable: one theme file, layouts that do not depend on After Harvest art (the
    later light set adds only the brand's own stitch and props) [ruling 2026-10-28].
35. Dark YouTube stage: near-black #1D1914, a gold spotlight behind the object, a 6% dot grid, eggshell type, a
    Literata italic subhead, one gold moment (#E8A100) per slide, the wrong answer faded and the right one lit, white
    cards under the light with a deep shadow [ruling 2026-10-28; slide-method §3b].
36. Light set: titles in three voices (Literata italic lead-in, Hanken Grotesk 900 shout, Olivera script), Plex Mono
    body, the After Harvest cross-stitch as the marks, harvest props, the straw hat, warm paper, sage and rust; full
    and a little playful, never tasteful and empty [ruling 2026-10-28; slide-method §3d].
37. Pick each layout from `templates/youtube/layouts.html` by its line's job, never the same twice in a row: L01 cold
    open, L02 the map, L03 chapter, L04 the turn, L05 the number, L06 step + screen, L07 side label (proof), L08
    split, L09 list, L10 quote, L11 story break, L12 reveal, L13 calendar, L14 end screen. Each is built around the
    camera, the face a third of the frame [ruling 2026-10-28; slide-method §3c].
38. The facecard sits in the same spot every slide, with things touching it and Sean's photo in previews; each slide
    is delivered alone, full size, no grey border or contact sheet [ruling 2026-10-28; slide-method §3, §4].
39. Never a template tell: the caps label / headline / italic line stack, a row of identical cards, the same shadow
    on everything, confetti, website parts rebuilt as boxes, invented copy, an italic accent word in every headline
    [slide-method §3d, §5].

**How Jason and Jeremy present (from the frames)**
40. The slide fills most of the screen with a small webcam picture-in-picture at the edge, moved so it never covers
    the highlight [Jason slides 1:09; Jeremy 23:56-24:26].
41. Hand marks made live, each colour one meaning: Jason's red loop on the biggest least-important thing, hatching on
    what to cut, arrows for reading direction, numbers for build order [Jason slides 1:39-1:59, 5:57, 11:44]; his pad's
    red verdict, green fix, black notes [Jason AI 1:09, 4:08]; Jeremy's black structure, blue ideals, red bad numbers,
    green money [Jeremy 1:29:56-1:32:26]. (Q5)
42. Real paper and artefacts as the visual: a printed sheet shot from above, hand in frame [Jason new webinar 0:59];
    his live pages, his email doc, a spreadsheet a red box walks down [Jeremy 2:09-5:59, 43:13].
43. A table that grows or returns: old vs new, back with the covered rows highlighted [Jason new webinar 6:35]; a
    from/to chart gaining one row per point, the finished chart as the recap [Jason new way 2:38-12:26].
44. A verdict and its fix in two fixed colours; the section title on screen the instant it is said [Jason AI 1:03-1:09,
    4:08, 17:21].
45. A real list with the current row lit as named [Jason AI 10:34-12:14]; one big typed total over its working [Jason
    AI 12:34]; a report card, a grade per option ("C+ MVP") [Jason AI 16:32].
46. The turn as two-tone type ("all is not / lost"); the anti-pitch as a struck-through list [Jason AI 0:29, 0:39].
47. One anchor illustration that keeps returning; the same diagram redrawn in a second colour to compare ideal and
    actual [Jeremy 8:18-21:26, 1:29:56].
48. "Later in this video" previews instead of an agenda slide, items not yet reached blurred [Jeremy 0:09-1:39]; a
    side sketch laid over the chart for one exercise, then removed [Jason new way 4:41-5:41].
49. Overlays almost never: caption bars only on the hook's credential numbers [Jason new way 0:04, 0:39]; a "Link in
    the description" pill on the spoken call to action [Jeremy 1:09:49]; no lower thirds in any of the five videos.

## How to do it

**One slide**: copy the line; write the belief, "How do we show this?" and the build; get the real thing; pick the
layout by the job; make the key words biggest and the title small top left; split to one idea; check rules 15 and 39.

**Break a crowded bullet slide** [Jason slides 13:43-15:22]
- Under a deadline: small title, regular bullets, bold lead-in words, no italics.
- Advanced: one point per slide, large in the centre; the finished points stack small top left, ticked.

**Run a mechanism slide live** [Jason slides 24:35-25:00]: first point biggest; when done, tick it and shrink it; the
next point becomes biggest; the one after is "a little opaque" as a tease; a coloured arrow marks the current step.

**The dramatic reveal** [Jason slides 31:00-31:48]: duplicate the slide; remove what is not needed; put a 100% solid
white box over what should fade, opacity about 80%; lay the new line on top.

**The intro tease** [Jason slides 2:48-4:38]: screenshot the later slides that teach each promised point ("very visual
at a glance"); one per slide under a small title, the words under it or in the notes; end on the cut-off wall.

**The call to action slide** [Jason slides 41:06-42:14]
```
Everything you get:                       Only available for: [countdown, never covered]
[item] .......... [value]                 [guarantee]
Total value [$X]  (big, bigger than       Your investment (small): [price, not giant]
                   the investment)
[ full-width yellow bar:  Get started now ->  [URL in blue type] ]
```
One call to action; no presenter photo; questions answered live, not on the slide.

**Title as the question** [Jason slides 26:05]: "If [they already know X], why [doesn't Y happen]?"

## Mistakes he calls out

- Form over function: it "looks really nice, but will also crush your conversions" [Jason slides 0:18].
- Numbers or a giant price as the biggest thing [Jason slides 35:13, 41:22]; ten bullets on one slide [Jason slides
  5:17].
- Stock, AI images, neon, glows, gears [Jason slides 5:56, 19:01, 23:31, 35:03]; AI slides "can't really edit"
  [Jason slides 37:39], though Claude "really focuses on the information" [Jason slides 25:24].
- Sean's rejections: "too much like Claude ... templateized", "messy", "boring af", "placeholders" [ruling
  2026-10-28].

## Lines worth keeping

> "They make the least important thing the biggest thing and therefore the center and focus of attention when it
> should be the other way around." [Jason slides 1:55]

> "Slides are free. So, we should use as many of them as we possibly can." [Jason slides 6:09]

> "Not metaphorically, not conceptually, not with AI generated images. Screenshots are your best friend on a
> webinar." [Jason slides 20:05]

> "The beauty of animation isn't because it looks cool. It's because it can focus the attention on the information
> sequentially." [Jason slides 21:18]

> "When people are on webinars, they care about information. They don't care about design." [Jason slides 40:15]

> "It's better to look ugly with design than it is to look pretty." [Jason slides 16:06]

> "It looks messy. It doesn't really look great... It doesn't look nicely made" [ruling 2026-10-28]

**Rulings 2026-10-29:** real pictures and real screenshots on every story or proof slide, our own first, stock as
a marked placeholder, each with a one-line source; marked with thread and pinned or sewn on, never taped; nothing
made in Canva (rules/slide-method.md 3e, 3f).

**Our reading** (ours): his rules are about function (what is biggest, what is real, what appears when), the rulings
about finish. Apply both.
