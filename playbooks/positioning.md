# Positioning playbook

Read this when: positioning the presenter, the product or the vehicle; the "why listen to me" part of a webinar, VSL or video; picking proof for slides; putting a client or partner forward as the star.
Sources: `sources/fladlien/notes/full-methodology.md` L4011-4822 (Jason Fladlien's Positioning Masterclass, from his GOAT Webinars training [doc L4805-4811]). Built 2026-10-29. The source is the master: re-check a quote there.

## In 5 lines
1. Position two things: you (nobody else can solve this for them) and the opportunity (why now, why the normal way fails, why your way wins).
2. Prove it with the most direct proof you have, shown "in the wild", and a lot of it.
3. Carve the market until you are first at something still worth being first at.
4. Take the position at the start, hold it through the content, extend it in the pitch.
5. Spend more time setting up each point than making it: the position of the point beats the point.

## The rules

**The two positions**
1. Position you: how you are uniquely qualified. His frame: "WITHOUT me, you have no hope." [doc L4019-4033]
2. Position the opportunity: why it is hot, why now, why the normal way falls short, why your way is the difference. Frame: "You're with me in this opportunity, you win. You're not, you lose." [doc L4035-4045]

**"I'm kind of a big deal": five elements that position you** [doc L4047-4061]
3. Proof: the most critical element (rules 8-11). [doc L4063]
4. Motive: why you are the one teaching this: backstory, journey, what drives you, why now, what is at stake. It makes authority human and believable. [doc L4195-4215]
5. Analogy: complex ideas grasped faster, remembered longer, connected. [doc L4217-4225]
6. Story: illustrates, connects emotionally, makes you memorable, carries lessons facts cannot. Weave personal, client and historical stories through every webinar. [doc L4245-4259]
7. Niched down: if you cannot be first overall, carve the market until you are first in something valuable (rules 12-13). [doc L4261-4265]

**Proof**
8. Strongest to weakest: direct results (yours: money made, pounds lost, tax saved), indirect results (stepping stones: more podcasts, more products live), secondary results (supporting metrics), customer results (proof by proxy), testimonials, accolades, visuals. [doc L4065-4129]
9. Show results "in the wild": the real platform's screen, a dashboard captured live on Zoom, the bank account. Never a spreadsheet, where "anybody can put any number". [doc L4083-4091, L4533-4541]
10. Quantity becomes its own quality: "Smear your slides with as much proof as possible." Most people could show 10x more in the same time. Layer different kinds of proof through the whole talk. [doc L4131-4143] (Our slide ruling changes how: see After Harvest.)
11. Proof does double duty: proof of a past sell-out makes future scarcity believable. [doc L4093-4097]

**Niching**
12. Carve by place ("first in Colorado"), who ("first for women over 50"), method, industry, result speed or a unique combination ("first to combine A + B + C"). Goal: honestly say "I'm the only one who can solve your problem this way." [doc L4267-4285]
13. Build on what is true; never force a square peg into a round hole. [doc L4499-4505]

**Four levels of self-positioning** [doc L4287-4311]
14. MVP: something valuable, presented competently. Baseline credibility (his example: QR Control).
15. Quick and great: stack many kinds of proof and authority fast (LinkedInception).
16. Big bopper: THE authority, heavy credentials, market leader (Product eClass).
17. Inception, the top level: position someone else as the star and be the cheerleader. "If I talk about me: blah. If YOU talk about me: let's go!" It is more believable, and even split, the pie is bigger. [doc L4309-4333]
18. Inception order: you as credible, then the opportunity as incredible, then the star. [doc L4315-4323]
19. Reveal the star early when you lack deep credentials in that niche (you are the guide introducing the master); late when you have credentials but the star has more ("I bow in the presence of greatness" right before the pitch). [doc L4359-4387]
20. Fit the positioning to the offer and to your real relationship with the expertise. [doc L4543-4567]

**Take, hold, extend** [doc L4403-4423]
21. Take the position at the start. Hold it through the content, never wavering or apologising for your authority. Extend it in the pitch: you are even cooler than they thought.
22. Choose a position that crushes the competition and makes people want to do business with you.
23. Assert it with as many of the five elements, as dramatically, as you can.

**Delivery and setup**
24. Be congruent: delivery and tone must match the position. Say "Today I'm here to talk to you about happiness" flatly and you're lost. [doc L4427-4433]
25. Own your edge and reputation: direct gets more direct, educational teaches deeper, inspirational amplifies. Don't be someone you're not. He now owns being a guru, even that people skip his webinars "because we always end up buying something!" [doc L4435-4449, L4569-4589]
26. Engineer the position: decide which one you want to hold, then build toward it. [doc L4451-4455]
27. The position of the point beats the point. The audience already knows most points and doesn't follow them; the setup, frame and journey to the point is the value. [doc L4457-4485]

**Desire, journey, impact**
28. Tie the position to one of three desires, in both frames: more money / not broke, look sexier / not ugly, better relationships / not bad ones. The negative frame is often stronger. "Everything goes back to one of those things." [doc L4591-4615]
29. Move the prospect from "I'm not gonna do business with anybody" to "ANYBODY who has it" to "only the LEGITIMATE folks" to "only YOU." [doc L4389-4401] In short: "I don't need this" > "I need this from someone" > "I need this from YOU specifically." [doc L4799-4801]
30. Impact, lowest to highest: facts alone; facts with some proof; proof plus you as credible; plus the opportunity as urgent; plus someone else as the star. [doc L4655-4665]

**Structure**
31. Teach as Russian nesting dolls: one paradigm (the thing that changes everything), 3-5 vital few points under it (the legs of the stool), 3 elements in each, each splitting into 3 more if needed. Works for any teaching. [doc L4145-4193] The paradigm layer: `playbooks/paradigm.md`.

## How to do it
1. Audit: what you are known for, what you can prove, what makes you different, your "Ed Viesturs moment" (the unique achievement). [doc L4705-4713]
2. Carve the niche (rule 12), then gather proof in rule 8's order, aiming for 10x what you have. [doc L4715-4737]
3. Write the motive; prepare 2-3 analogies and a story for each point; lay the teaching out as nesting dolls. [doc L4739-4753]
4. Consider Inception: someone to make the star, or a joint venture where you present and they are the expert? [doc L4755-4761]
5. Engineer the position, stay congruent, own your edge, and set up each point before revealing it. [doc L4763-4779]
6. Check: you have every proof type of rule 8, a motive, analogies, stories and a niche; the opportunity has why now, why the normal way fails, why yours wins and the win/lose frame. [doc L4619-4653]

Templates (his frames, blanks ours)
- "You could go on 100 other [webinars] from 100 other people and you're screwed when it comes to [this specific thing]. Nobody's gonna do it better than [me]." [doc L4027-4031]
- Inception: "The more [webinars] I do... the more businesses knock on my door... my answer is almost always NO... I'm not interested in great. I'm interested in GREATEST. I'm lucky if I find one thing a year that passes my BFF test." Then the star passes it. [doc L4335-4357]
- BFF test: "If my best friend in the world came to me and he was limited on money and time, and had to absolutely make money or his whole world would collapse, what would I recommend to him?" [doc L4351-4355]

## Examples
- The $1M webinar: 100 spots at $10,000, sold out in under 60 minutes; he shows Will's text telling him to STOP selling. [doc L4093-4097]
- Ed Viesturs: first American to summit all 14 8,000-metre peaks without supplemental oxygen. "Firsts" keep getting carved smaller ("the first person with one extra nipple to climb Everest"): pick where you are first. [doc L4227-4243]
- Manuel Suarez (supplements): no supplement credentials, so Jason makes Manuel the star from the start. [doc L4313-4357, L4547-4553]
- Dan and Dylan (Wholesale Formula): Jason builds his own Amazon credibility all webinar, then bows to them right before the pitch. [doc L4383-4387, L4555-4561]
- Trey wouldn't share results; Jason took screenshots on a Zoom, used them anyway and told him later. Always ask: how many ways can I show this result? [doc L4515-4531]
- "Calories in, calories out" is the point everyone knows; said plainly, "They'll gain MORE weight to spite you." [doc L4461-4465]

## Mistakes he calls out
- "Punchline, punchline, punchline": no setup, just information dumping. [doc L4477-4481]
- A flat delivery that doesn't match the expert position. [doc L4429-4431]
- Wavering or apologising for your authority. [doc L4409]
- One kind of proof only, or proof on a spreadsheet anyone could fake. [doc L4141-4143, L4535-4537]
- Faking the position, or trying to be someone you're not. [doc L4501-4505, L4589]
