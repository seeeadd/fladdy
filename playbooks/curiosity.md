# Curiosity playbook

Read this when: building curiosity, teasing what is coming, open loops, the agenda or "on this webinar" part (of a webinar, a video intro or the VSL), writing blind bullets, moving from one section to the next.
Sources: `sources/fladlien/notes/full-methodology.md` L1965-3320 (Jason Fladlien, "The Tease (Complete Guide)"). Built 2026-10-29. The source is the master: re-check a quote there.

## In 5 lines
1. Tease the whole way through, not only in the intro: give the outcome, hide the how.
2. Make every tease specific (exact numbers, timeframes, outcomes); vague hype is empty.
3. Six techniques: blind bullets, questions, partial reveal, open mind, my goal, friction. Layer 2-3 on each slide.
4. Reveal one line at a time, and redirect with "first..." or "more on that in a second", never "but".
5. If people only stay for a bribe, fix the content.

## The rules

**The idea**
1. Teasing creates curiosity and anticipation without fully revealing what you will share: it keeps people engaged, opens loops you close later and makes them stay for the answers. [doc L2029-2041]
2. Tease throughout, like romance through the whole courtship. Never reveal everything on the first date. [doc L2043-2047, L3209]
3. Study the techniques in isolation, apply them blended: the best slides use 2-3 at once. Ask "what's the most effective way to communicate this?", not "where does technique #3 go?" [doc L2015-2027, L3073-3075]
4. Nothing important comes out of nowhere. Build up to it with questions (the need), analogies (understanding) and teasers (anticipation). [doc L3115-3125]
5. Specificity creates believability: exact numbers ("3 word sentence", "99.48%"), timeframes ("in 90 days"), outcomes ("$10,000 plus expenses paid"). [doc L3127-3137]
6. Animate: reveal one item at a time so nobody reads bullet 7 while you explain bullet 2. Each reveal is a small "what's next?" gap. [doc L3139-3169]

**The six techniques**
7. On this webinar (blind bullets): the outcome without the how, made believable with specific numbers. Written last: "If you can't write these, hire somebody to write them." [doc L2051-2069, L2183-2185]
8. Questions (faux interaction): the questions they should be asking. Bullets make them a student (desire to learn); questions make them feel behind (anxiety about what they are missing). [doc L2187-2215]
9. Partial reveal (the most advanced and most riveting): show the framework or criteria, never the details. Enough to understand, not enough to implement. [doc L2301-2311]
10. Open mind: challenge what they think they know. For the biggest claims, cynical markets, a counterintuitive mechanism, and the start of the webinar. [doc L2449-2459, L2519-2527]
11. My goal: say plainly what you are doing, including that you will sell. It raises trust: no "when's the pitch coming" tension, and it respects their intelligence. [doc L2545-2555, L2631-2639]
12. Friction: show it is harder than it looks. Push-pull: "think seduction". [doc L2641-2653]

**Supporting tactics**
13. It gets better: just as they think "okay, that's good", raise the stakes. 2-4 times a webinar (after strong results, before major teaching, before the offer); more and it loses impact. [doc L2773-2807]
14. Before we dive in: an easy open loop that controls the pace, to establish credibility, set context, clear objections or delay gratification (delay means more desire). [doc L2809-2855]
15. Analogies: the more foreign the concept, the better the analogy. Spend the hours on 2-3 for your hardest concepts. A good one makes the scary feel safe, the complex simple, the foreign familiar; if they are still confused, keep looking. [doc L2857-2861, L2915-2925, L3225]
16. Giveaways: almost never. Only something that "drops from the heavens": truly special, not another ebook or video, genuinely wanted, scarce. Test: would you personally want it? If not, make better content. [doc L2927-2965]
17. Transitions: show something exciting, redirect just as they want to dive in, promise to come back, teach the foundation, then deliver. [doc L2967-2971, L3005-3015]
18. Avoid "but" in transitions: it invalidates everything before it. Use "first..." or "however..." [doc L2989-2995]

## How to do it

**Blind bullets** [doc L2061-2069, L2085-2181, L3079-3085]
Go through the finished webinar, extract each teaching, write it as an outcome with the method hidden. Mix what to do with what not to do. The more specific, the more real, and the less they can work it out alone ("12 most searched keywords": they can't Google that).
- "The [number] [thing] that [specific outcome]"
- "The [number] you should do and the [number] you shouldn't"
- "[Strategies NOT aimed to get X]... but [do it anyway]" (a paradox)
- "How to [result] without [common obstacle]"
- An exact percentage, never "most"
- "[bullet] ([extra intrigue])"
- "Why [counterintuitive statement] and [how to fix it]"

**Questions** [doc L2221-2235, L2289-2299, L3087-3095]
"Here are the questions you should be asking..." then 3-5 that escalate, simple mixed with complex, objections voiced as questions ("Did you miss the gold rush period?"), then "I have answers... In fact, better answers than you'll find anywhere!" Use them to fix misconceptions, get a yes ("Would you make a valid effort...?") and stop "I can do this myself".

**Partial reveal** [doc L2337-2343, L2357-2369, L2431-2447]
Show the criteria, not the details ("the 3 criteria for perfect keywords", never "use these 12 keywords"). A last line like "If you think you figured it out, you're missing the most important part" stops "oh, I know this". Test: they feel "I get what makes this work" and "but I don't know how to actually do it". Too much and they try it alone; too little and they doubt you have anything.

**Open mind** [doc L2373-2405, L2529-2543]
Voice their cynicism, admit it seems too good to be true, ask only that they stay open minded, promise they decide for themselves, then present your case. "You might be thinking... [4-5 cynical thoughts]" / "You are hopefully also thinking... maybe it IS possible with the right approach?" / "That's all I'm asking, just be open minded... Then make your own conclusions, cool?" Acknowledge the resistance and ask permission; once they give it, they listen.

**My goal** [doc L2557-2569, L2603-2609, L2621-2629]
"I'm going to give you [the outcome]... with the hope that it excites you so much you'll invest in what I have to offer at the end... and if that's the case, I have a special deal for early action takers!" Or as a transformation: "My goal: to get you from '[where they are stuck]' to '[the aha]'." Best early, for cynical or advanced audiences, or when you are positioned as the leader.

**Friction** [doc L2727-2769, L3105-3111]
Show the possibility (excitement), reveal the complexity (tension), position your solution (relief), then the pull again: it requires [investment or action]. Forms: "This seems [easy]... it's actually [hard as hell]"; a vision, then "however", then the obstacle; the big earners, then "here's who you should emulate". It stops "I can do this myself", creates urgency ("limiting timeframe", "once in a lifetime") and shows you understand the challenge. Never so much they feel hopeless.

**Transition lines** [doc L2839-2845, L2973-3003]
"More on that in a second, first..." (his favourite rhythm) / "Does it get better? Yeah, it gets better. However..." / "Now..." / "We'll talk about that later, first we have to..." / "Hold on, before I show you that..."

**Before it ships** [doc L3219-3243, L3271]
Would this make me want to know more? Too much given away, or too vague? 2-3 techniques layered? To practise blind bullets: Gary Halbert's newsletter "The Twist".

## Examples
- LinkedIn Inception: "The 3 word sentence you MUST put on your LinkedIn profile (this opens and closes deals for you automatically)" and "10 words you should never put on your LinkedIn Profile (chances are at least one is killing deals right now)". [doc L2071-2083]
- QR Control: "a certain type of Mobile Marketing Strategy... That has been - up until now - completely ignored... And the best prospects are hiding in plain sight." Four techniques in three bullets. [doc L3025-3045]
- Affiliorama: questions under each step of a simple 3-step process show its hidden depth. Opportunities that work "even at 10% effective implementation", "low downside, high upside": "the combination lock without the combination". [doc L2247-2287, L2315-2335]
- ASM4: "Is the following hype? I'm going to show you how to build a MILLION DOLLAR plus business in less than 12 months... And that's not even the best part!" [doc L2461-2479]
- WP Monster: "However, my chief goal IS to sell you something at the end..." [doc L2583-2591]
- ASM3 Raul: people doing $20K-$100K+ a month, then "here is who you should emulate": Raul, about $3,500 net a month. The burning "why?" holds them. [doc L2677-2703]
- Launchpad: a vision of worldwide distribution, then "HOWEVER... CASH IS KING". [doc L2705-2725]
- Analogies: crypto bots as a tenant who pays rent every day, so volatility reads as daily cash flow (two hours of work, worth it for a $2K-$10K program); SEO as a "safe cracker society" that brute forces the combination and shares it. [doc L2863-2913]
- Giveaway: a replica of Zig Ziglar's dog-eared copy of "Over the Top"; people texted to enter, which captured phone numbers. [doc L2949-2959]

## Mistakes he calls out
- Coming out like a blunt object: facts with no mystery. [doc L3201]
- Bribing people to stay. [doc L2933-2935, L3203]
- Giving away too much, or being too vague: "I'm going to share something amazing" is empty hype. [doc L3205-3207, L3213]
- Teasing in the intro, then boring content. [doc L3209]
- "But" in transitions. [doc L3211]
- So much friction they feel hopeless; overusing "it gets better". [doc L2767-2769, L2807]
- Bad dating: too strong at once, everything in the first 5 minutes, commitment before attraction. [doc L3175-3183]
