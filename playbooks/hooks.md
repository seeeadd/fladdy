# Hooks playbook

Read this when: writing a webinar or video hook, a YouTube title, the first line of an ad, a VSL opening.
Sources: `sources/fladlien/notes/full-methodology.md` L5-1167 (Jason Fladlien's Hook Mastery and its AfterHarvest
applications); open questions also cite two teardowns. Built 2026-10-29. The source is the master: re-check a quote
there.

## In 5 lines
1. A hook wins favorable, qualified attention that primes them to buy. Attention alone fails. [doc L11-23]
2. Start with one of the 7 best hooks, the pop quiz first; add complexity later. [doc L547-561, L639-645]
3. Every number gets a comparison or a "which means". [doc L497-521]
4. Write the first 30 seconds (about 100 words) for the cold replay viewer. [doc L411-439]
5. Remove the limit that stops them buying, and prove every big claim. [doc L347-369, L189-191]

## The rules

**What a hook is**
1. A hook gains attention that is favorable (the right attention), qualified (the right sub-segment) and increases
   purchasing (prejudices them toward buying). [doc L11-17]
2. Prime them for the sale: "Free sex and beer" gets attention and loses it the moment you pivot. [doc L19-23]
3. Aim at one sub-segment, as narrow as possible, yet the fattest and most profitable one. [doc L25-31]
4. Use some of the 12 elements, not all: novel, shocking, engaging, controversial, specific, exciting, dramatic,
   unpredictable, bold, emotional, urgent, topical. The checklist asks for 3 or more. [doc L33-65, L1121]

**The 7 best hooks**
5. Pop quiz: interactive questions that reveal surprising facts, prove their assumptions wrong and show scale
   without claiming it. [doc L69-81]
6. Pitch up front: say exactly what you sell in the first 60 seconds. No bait and switch, and the wrong people
   leave early. It can be the whole frame. [doc L101-113, L131]
7. Fortune teller: predict their future on the current path vs the new one; it shows the cost of doing nothing.
   [doc L135-147]
8. Hype but true: an outrageous claim that sounds like hype, then prove it, or lose all credibility. [doc L169-191]
9. Tall task: a challenge that seems impossible, then help them do it. It attacks resistance. [doc L199-211]
10. Immediate expectation: tell them what happens right now in bold, direct language; you are in control,
    with a lot to cover. [doc L231-245]
11. Benefit, benefit, benefit: stack benefits fast. Done beats better, hope then pain, urgent tone. [doc L265-275]

**The 7 edge case hooks** (less common, strong when they fit) [doc L305-321]
12. Pain, pain, pain: stack the pain of not acting.
13. Left field: an unexpected angle or analogy.
14. Intriguing question: an open loop that demands an answer.
15. Pressure cooker: a time-sensitive scenario.
16. Recent development: "new discovery", "just announced".
17. Fill in the blanks: build a framework with them.
18. Between us: insider, confidential framing.

**Choosing one**
19. Tier 1, works almost anywhere: pop quiz, pitch up front, fortune teller. Tier 2: hype but true (needs proof),
    tall task (an actionable challenge), immediate expectation (confident positioning). Tier 3: benefit stack
    (transactional offers), edge cases. Start at tier 1, pop quiz above all. [doc L613-645]
20. Start simple: master one hook as is, then combine elements, then build hooks before products. [doc L547-561]
21. Isolate elements from application: ask which elements make a hook work (pop quiz: interaction, pattern
    interrupt, comparison, objection handling), then recombine them. [doc L325-345]

**Principles**
22. Remove limits: people buy when limits are removed more than when gain is shown. Attack objections as fast as
    possible; remove the blocking belief. [doc L347-369]
23. Hook before product: find compelling proof, a story or an angle, then build the product around it. "Proof before
    product." [doc L371-409]
24. Manufacture hooks: design proof (show everyone's results), exclusivity (the limit is the hook) or authority by
    association (Ramp Engine partnered with Trey Lewellen for his story) into the business. [doc L563-595, L1051]
25. The replay needs the stronger hook: the live room is warm, the replay viewer cold. [doc L411-439]
26. Two hooks: the one that gets them to the webinar (curiosity, registration) need not be the webinar's own
    (proof, credibility, buying mindset). [doc L441-457]
27. Calibrate: learn their language and beliefs, mirror their terms, reference shared experiences, meet them where
    they are, then lead. [doc L459-495]
28. Statistics need a comparison: number plus human comparison, percentage plus implication. [doc L497-521]
29. References must be current for this audience. [doc L539-545]
30. Spend real time on the hook: it is the foundation. [doc L1011-1013]
31. Test, don't guess: track which hook gets responses. [doc L1019-1021]
32. Interaction beats presentation: get them typing and answering. [doc L1035-1037]
33. When it works, go hard and scale. [doc L597-611]

**Pop quiz questions** [doc L523-537]
34. Not all easy: let them get some wrong so they think "I need to learn this." [doc L91-93]
35. Each question builds toward one point.
36. Escalating numbers show scale.
37. Make them type answers.
38. Reveal quickly: "Boom! The answer is..."
39. Add comparisons.

**Ready checklist** [doc L1113-1135]
40. Favorable attention? Repels the wrong people? Primes buying? 3+ elements? Sayable in 30 seconds? Removes an
    objection? Their language? Proof behind it? Simple enough to test? Can Thea deliver it? Seven ticks: deploy.

## How to do it
1. Watch 10 webinars in your space and note each one's hook type. Name your sub-segment, collect its words and beliefs.
   [doc L1055-1063]
2. Pick one tier-1 hook (pop quiz if unsure), draft from a template below, run the ready checklist.
3. Write 3 versions, test on 5 people, send 20 Looms, A/B test, double down, then make variants per channel.
   [doc L1053-1093]
4. Live: small talk, then "I'm going to take a deep breath. I'd love for you to join me right now. Breathe in with me
   now. Hold it. Release. Let's begin." Script all after "Let's begin", start the replay there, tune its first 100
   words. [doc L417-429]

Templates:
- Impossible unless (Dr. Alan Barnard): "[This] seems impossible, unless you have [X]. I have [X], here's proof."
  [doc L115-121]
- Fortune teller: "Here's where you're headed: [pain]. Here's where you could be: [pleasure]. Here's the one thing
  that decides which path you take: [X]. Let me show you how to guarantee path 2." [doc L157-165]
- Tall task: "Your challenge: [measurable result] in [time] using only [what we teach today]. Who's in?" Admit it
  sounds hard, show it is doable, give the roadmap, celebrate. [doc L213-227]
- Benefit stack: 5-7 tangible benefits (not features), paced; "But here's the problem most people face..."; the
  solution. [doc L291-301]
- Statistic: "[Number]. That's [human comparison]." / "[Percentage]. That means [implication]." [doc L503-521]

## Examples
- Pop quiz, YouTube Secrets: 1 billion+ visitors a month, 70% outside the US, top channel 13 million. [doc L83-89]
- "6 billion hours watched" means nothing until "there are only 7 billion people on earth". [doc L95-97]
- Fortune teller: Affiliorama's quiz predicted 6 months on each path and the gap. [doc L149-155]
- Hype but true, built before the product: all 18 one-on-one students' portfolios shown live. [doc L193-195, L569-575]
- Immediate expectation: "Some of you will get uncomfortable because you're making these mistakes right now. Good."
  [doc L255-261]
- Calibration: a numerology list got "this is the chat box", "web-based training" (not "webinar") and his own
  numerology number; Bob Proctor's audience got "wealth potential". [doc L461-483]

## Mistakes he calls out
- Attention that doesn't lead to the sale; a big claim without proof. [doc L21-23, L189-191]
- Numbers with no comparison: "meaningless". [doc L499-501]
- Showing $10K a month to someone who thinks they can't make $100. [doc L365]
- A crazy hook for a list that has never seen a webinar; dated references. [doc L461-465, L539-545]
- Trying to be Jason right away; a product in search of a hook. [doc L561, L383]

## After Harvest
From the doc (written for a retention offer to course creators):
- Five hooks written for that offer: a pop quiz of 5 multiple-choice questions, each reveal made a point; two
  futures at 6, 12 and 18 months; pitch up front with the price and who it is not for; a "97% completion" hype but
  true; a 30-day retention sprint. [doc L651-773]
- The Second Harvest as pop quiz, hype but true (math, live) and left field (a field you already planted, left to
  rot). [doc L775-817]
- Opening, minutes 0-12: breath and a chat question, pop quiz, fortune teller, Second Harvest table; then proof,
  process, pitch. [doc L819-853]
- Calibration words, five objections with answers, the proof needed, hooks per channel. [doc L855-1007]
- The path: pop quiz tested on 10 ideal clients by Loom (aim 30%+ replies), then fortune teller, hype but true, an
  authority story. Suggested hook: "The 10% Problem: Why 90% of Your Students Are Failing (And How to Fix It in 90
  Days)". [doc L897-933, L1137-1163]

Our reading: the frames carry over to 3-day events, but the numbers, offer and words must be redone from our own
facts, each with a source (rule 8). No ruling changes this topic yet.
