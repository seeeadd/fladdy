# YouTube playbook

Read this when: planning, scripting or structuring a YouTube video, a video series, or the "core video".
Sources: the teardowns `jason-fladlien-videos/02 The New Webinar (VtXp0UwwK3Y)/teardown.md` ([Jason new webinar m:ss]),
`03 This is the New Way to Sell (VStitVKO1AI)/teardown.md` ([Jason new way m:ss]) and `04 AI is mostly Bullshit
(v2yNV7rkyzI)/teardown.md` ([Jason AI m:ss]), each read whole; `rulings.md`. Built 2026-10-29. The source is the
master: re-check a quote there.

## In 5 lines

1. Teaching is the sale: teach first, and run launches, challenges and webinars on the attention it earns [Jason new
   way 1:48-2:27].
2. One unlisted core video (one to three hours) explains everything and how you work with clients; every other video
   ends by pointing to it [Jason new webinar 12:31, 13:13].
3. Mine the other videos from your webinar slides, one slide unpacked for 10 minutes each; most buyers need about
   three [Jason new webinar 14:05, 16:19].
4. Sell soft and throughout, with more than one kind of call to action; no stack, no invented scarcity [Jason new
   webinar 7:18-7:54, 10:02, 11:08].
5. Teach few ideas many ways: least info to change behaviour, why / what / how / what if, one operation an outsider
   could check [Jason new way 3:11, 6:26, 7:34, 12:08].

## The rules

**The model**
1. Shift "from selling as teaching to teaching as selling": teaching is the primary way to sell; the webinar then sells
   to people who already trust and understand you, "when you're ready" [Jason new way 1:48-2:27].
2. "The thing has now become more valuable than the perception of the thing" [Jason new way 0:39-0:54].
3. Four points: a more informed buyer buys better; create independence and you get dependence; the less you need the
   sale, the easier it is; the top 1% holds 50% of a market's value, won by "empowerment and referral" [Jason new way
   1:15-1:44].
4. A YouTube webinar is "a webinar that doesn't look like a webinar": long-form, no registration, no Zoom, no visible
   pitch. The scroller wants information "but they don't want to be sold" [Jason new webinar 0:30-2:34].
5. Change the traditional webinar element by element. Its fifteen: hook, pain, gain, position, paradigm, mechanisms,
   demonstration, recap, tie-down, choices, core, price, bonus, guarantee, scarcity [Jason new webinar 0:49-1:21].

**Old webinar to YouTube webinar**
6. Hook: "The previous video" brings them; "The packaging isn't important"; the core video is usually unlisted [Jason
   new webinar 2:36-3:51]. (Q7)
7. Opener: no pop quiz, never "write it down in the YouTube comments"; the YouTube two agendas instead (template
   below) [Jason new webinar 3:53-5:55].
8. Position the vehicle, not you: no 5-10 minutes of credentials; "Look at this thing that we use" and why it beats
   anything else for this problem [Jason new webinar 6:04-6:51]. (Q8)
9. "Soft sell throughout", more than one call to action of more than one kind: link in the description (offer doc,
   landing page, funnel), pinned comment, DM; text or WhatsApp to get personal [Jason new webinar 6:51-8:11].
10. These videos reach "1% of the market that spends 40% of its money", so high-touch contact pays; repetition moves
    them [Jason new webinar 8:13-8:47].
11. The product is demonstrated throughout, every deliverable in action, with the client / not-client line [Jason new
    webinar 9:08-9:58].
12. No offer stack on the video: it sits behind the link, "pretty low-key" [Jason new webinar 9:58-10:27].
13. Scarcity is built into the offer ("Richard Mille style": a fixed number, a release rate, reachable only through
    this path) and kept; never "buy before the end" [Jason new webinar 10:25-11:46].
14. The flywheel: fixed supply gives a reliable number and a content budget (25 x $2,000 = $50,000 a month); grow
    demand, keep supply, double the price [Jason new webinar 11:46-12:21].

**The funnel**
15. The core video: unlisted, one to three hours, "better to be longer than shorter", calls to action throughout; it
    shows how you work with clients and the resources you built, how to make them yourself or get them from you.
    "It's better to show more than to show less" [Jason new webinar 12:22-13:11].
16. Every other video is an on-ramp ending on a line to the core video [Jason new webinar 13:11-13:36].
17. Work backwards: which mindsets make someone ready for the core video, and what content is useful alone but moves
    them toward it [Jason new webinar 13:39-13:59].
18. One slide, one video: "if this one slide could be unpacked for 10 minutes, what would I say?", from the
    50,000-ft view or the weeds; feeders can feed the feeders [Jason new webinar 14:01-15:05].
19. About three videos before most buy, and 100 would still be worth it; old videos keep selling (200-500 views, 1-3
    sales a month): "a fine wine" [Jason new webinar 16:19-16:55].

**Teaching that sells** (his challenges: old habit to new)
20. Inform to transform: "least info needed to change behavior"; value is whether "a person can no longer operate in a
    limited capacity"; often teach less [Jason new way 2:44-3:52].
21. Content to context: "Move away from this and move toward that" [Jason new way 3:55-4:34].
22. Objective best to subjective best: teach the option with the lower compliance threshold (often the third best),
    for the market's current consciousness [Jason new way 4:34-6:17].
23. Quantity to clarity: a few concepts, many ways [Jason new way 6:17-6:54].
24. Singular to modal: every point as why, what, how and what if [Jason new way 6:54-8:12].
25. Logic to wisdom: heart alone gets "a lot of praise, but no buyers", head alone changes no behaviour [Jason new way
    8:33-9:43].
26. Instruction plus commitment, asked before and after: "It's not presentation, it's conversation" [Jason new way
    9:43-10:41].
27. Fundamentals plus novelty: "If you want a new idea, read an old book", right-sized with your own touch [Jason new
    way 10:41-11:50].
28. Theory to application: "operations to perform" that a third-party auditor could check [Jason new way 11:50-12:29].
29. Teach as you sell, openly: the best do-it-yourself lesson, plus "if at any point in time you want help, we have
    resources"; remind often, since it is "selling for service" [Jason new way 12:29-13:28].

**A contrarian video**
30. Open in about 70 s with the nine beats below [Jason AI 0:00-1:09].
31. Build it as verdict / fix pairs: each part opens on a spoken verdict and turns to "instead" inside the part [Jason
    AI 1:03-30:31].
32. Prove each pair with your own numbers and one outside source [Jason AI 1:10, 6:17, 12:34, 27:24].
33. Keep it moving: open loops, provocation announced first, a guess asked before a number, a signpost for the last
    part, self-mockery, one plain analogy per point [Jason AI 2:25, 5:45, 15:30, 23:06].
34. "Nothing is dead or alive": a tactic's value depends on the market's context [Jason AI 2:48-2:53].
35. Keep the anti-pitch you promise in the hook [Jason AI 0:36].

**How he sells and shoots**
36. Credentials in the first minute: $5K an hour plus one client number [Jason new webinar 0:00-0:30; Jason new way
    0:18-0:39; Jason AI 0:39-0:55].
37. One mid-roll ad ("Hey, real quick"), shot in another set, "information is in the bio", after a section's payoff,
    about halfway [Jason new webinar 8:47; Jason new way 8:12; Jason AI 14:27].
38. No subscribe ask or end card: he ends on a next step, a commitment or "Thoughts?" [Jason new webinar 15:48; Jason
    new way 13:37; Jason AI 31:34].
39. Mostly on camera (75-92%), reframed or cut every 5-20 s, one printed or handwritten visual, overlays only on the
    hook's numbers [Jason new webinar 0:59; Jason new way 0:04; Jason AI 0:29]. Details: `playbooks/slides.md`.

**AI on the scripts**
40. AI as a maker is weak (email copy about C+, under 2% of his reels AI-scripted); as a miner it wins [Jason AI
    14:49-17:37]. (Q9)
41. Micro, not macro: one section per prompt, a person checking each; "If it's losing the context, you are asking it
    to do too much" [Jason AI 9:51, 13:32-13:47, 14:21].
42. AI formalises an existing process, never invents one; "plug out the AI, plug in the human" [Jason AI 21:44,
    31:19].

## How to do it

**Build the funnel** [Jason new webinar 12:22-15:05]
1. Make the core video, unlisted, with a link, a pinned comment and a DM call to action.
2. End every other video on a line to it.
3. Work backwards to the mindsets and stand-alone topics that lead there.
4. Mine each webinar slide into a 10-minute video, from more than one angle; add feeders for the feeders.

**Study another channel** [Jason new webinar 15:15-15:45]: what do they link at the end? Listed or unlisted? How did
the previous video set it up? Pinned comments, links, DMs, phone numbers, an opt-in with sales follow-up?

**The YouTube two agendas** [Jason new webinar 5:21]: "I'm going to break it down for you exactly how we're successful
with [thing] and how we're helping so many other people get [result] and I'm going to show you how we can work
together in different ways throughout the video."

**The demonstration line** [Jason new webinar 9:24]: "This is the resource that we created that helps our clients do
[this]. If you're one of our clients, this is how we apply it for you. If you aren't our client, this is how you can
create your own version of this resource."

**The on-ramp ending** [Jason new webinar 13:29]: "If you want to see how we do this for clients, check out this
video."

**One point, four perspectives** [Jason new way 7:34-7:55]: "Here's why this is important. Here's what's involved.
Here's how to use it. And here's what could happen if it goes well for you. And here's what you can adjust if it
doesn't go well for you."

**The commitment loop** [Jason new way 9:51-10:22]: before, "If I show it to you and it resonates with you, then
commit to me that you [will] make a go of it ... Is that fair?"; after, "Can you see how this can help you in [this
way]? And will you commit to me to actually making this show up in your life?"

**Daily checks** [Jason new way 6:41, 9:35]: "How can I communicate the same thing in a slightly different way?" Too
logical, add emotion; too emotional, back it with logic.

**The contrarian hook** [Jason AI 0:00-1:00]: 1) a confession you could profit from [the hype]; 2) the problem as a
picture; 3) a betrayal they already felt; 4) the pain; 5) the turn, "But my friend, all is not lost"; 6) the
anti-pitch, "without selling you [X] at the end of this video"; 7) two credential numbers; 8) the bridge, "it can't
replace [part 1]. Which is where we're going to start"; 9) the first verdict, the outside proof on screen. Each part
after: "[X] is [verdict]." ... "Instead, [fix]."

**Research, angles, clips** [Jason AI 4:19-4:38, 17:29-20:44]: have AI summarise about 50 videos on a topic, write your
bets before you watch a couple, then check them. For angles: "Give me 50 different examples of how this idea can show
up"; keep the 1 good, ask for 50 more. Have AI count the clips in each long recording (40-45) and hold editors to it.

## Examples

- "I will never sell more than 25" at $2,000: $50,000 a month and a fixed content budget [Jason new webinar
  11:46-12:03].
- Five ways ranked: the third best has the lower compliance threshold, so people adopt it; "This is why A+ students
  work for C- bosses" [Jason new way 4:44-6:08].
- Outside proof per part: the HBR "trendslop" article, Israetel's AI-written book at 1.9 on Amazon [Jason AI 1:10,
  6:17].
- A 10x move over copy tweaks: a guarantee so strong ("we'll buy your business and pay you $25,000 on top") that "the
  funnel can still be dog [ __ ]" [Jason AI 24:15-24:46].
- One analogy per point: shovels and gold, the bucket brigade, ten rabbits, a hammer not a screwdriver [Jason AI 0:00,
  9:24, 23:44, 31:01].

## Mistakes he calls out

- Porting the live webinar as is: a hard pitch, a stack, end-of-webinar bonuses on a static video [Jason new webinar
  2:06-2:27, 10:25]; comment questions as an opener: "You're going to look like an idiot" [Jason new webinar 4:25].
- "The old guru way": informing, teaching the objective best, many concepts, one modality, theory, hiding the sale
  [Jason new way 1:52-13:28].
- AI slop "that looks good, but isn't good" made his clients worse at webinars [Jason AI 13:58-14:10]; long chats rot
  [Jason AI 7:42]; agents built on agents [Jason AI 9:14, 25:51].
- Chasing increments: "If you try to chase 10 rabbits at once, you go hungry" [Jason AI 23:29-23:44].

  stack slides stay in webinar decks and the Day 3 close; scarcity only if real (builds a month, a launch window)

  less you need the sale" [Jason new way 2:18, 1:30].

  video leaves one checkable operation [Jason new way 7:34, 3:11, 12:16].
- The contrarian hook for our openers, with a hype our viewers were sold and Sean's own numbers [Jason AI 0:00-1:09].
- C+ AI copy fits "an email where none exists" (the pre-event sequence), never the Day 3 close [Jason AI 27:14].

**Our reading** (ours): for After Harvest "the vehicle" is the 3-day event, so positioning the vehicle is Sean's
