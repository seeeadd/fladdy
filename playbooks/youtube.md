# YouTube playbook

Read this when: planning, scripting or structuring a YouTube video, a video series, or the "core video".
Sources: the teardowns `jason-fladlien-videos/02 The New Webinar (VtXp0UwwK3Y)/teardown.md` ([Jason new webinar m:ss]),
`03 This is the New Way to Sell (VStitVKO1AI)/teardown.md` ([Jason new way m:ss]) and `04 AI is mostly Bullshit
(v2yNV7rkyzI)/teardown.md` ([Jason AI m:ss]), each read whole; `rulings.md`. Built 2026-10-29. The source is the
master: re-check a quote there.

## In 5 lines

1. Teaching is the sale: teach first, and run launches, challenges and webinars on the attention it earns [Jason new
   way 1:48-2:27].
2. One unlisted core video (one to three hours) explains everything and how you work with clients; every other video
   ends by pointing to it [Jason new webinar 12:31, 13:13].
3. Mine the other videos from your webinar slides, one slide unpacked for 10 minutes each; most buyers need about
   three [Jason new webinar 14:05, 16:19].
4. Sell soft and throughout, with more than one kind of call to action; no stack and no invented scarcity on the
   video [Jason new webinar 7:18-7:54, 10:02, 11:08].
5. Teach few ideas many ways: least info to change behaviour, why / what / how / what if, one operation an outsider
   could check [Jason new way 3:11, 6:26, 7:34, 12:08].

## The rules

**The model**
1. Shift "from selling as teaching to teaching as selling": teaching is the primary way to sell, and the webinar then
   sells to people who already trust and understand you, "when you're ready" [Jason new way 1:48-2:27].
2. Education "has exceeded marketing": "The thing has now become more valuable than the perception of the thing"
   [Jason new way 0:39-0:54].
3. Four points: a more informed buyer buys better; create independence and you get dependence; the less you need the
   sale, the easier it is; the top 1% holds 50% of a market's value, won by "empowerment and referral" through
   education [Jason new way 1:15-1:44].
4. A YouTube webinar is "a webinar that doesn't look like a webinar": long-form, no registration, no Zoom, no visible
   pitch. The scroller wants information but not to be sold; hard selling belongs in private, not in public or in
   what the algorithm rewards [Jason new webinar 0:30-2:34].
5. Change the traditional webinar one element at a time ("the differences of media"). Its fifteen elements: hook,
   pain, gain, position, paradigm, mechanisms, demonstration, recap, tie-down, choices, core, price, bonus, guarantee,
   scarcity [Jason new webinar 0:49-1:21, 2:34].

**Old webinar to YouTube webinar**
6. Hook: none needed. "The previous video" brings them; "The packaging isn't important"; the core video is usually
   unlisted [Jason new webinar 2:36-3:51]. (Q8)
7. Introduction: no pop quiz, never "write it down in the YouTube comments" (a "social faux pas"); open with the
   YouTube two agendas (template below) [Jason new webinar 3:53-5:55].
8. Positioning: the vehicle, not you. Skip the 5-10 minutes of credentials; "Look at this thing that we use" and why
   it beats anything else for this problem [Jason new webinar 6:04-6:51]. (Q9)
9. Selling: "soft sell throughout", more than one call to action of more than one kind: link in the description (an
   offer doc, landing page or funnel), pinned comment, DM; a number to text or WhatsApp if you want it personal [Jason
   new webinar 6:51-8:11].
10. These videos reach "1% of the market that spends 40% of its money", so high-touch contact pays; repetition moves
    them [Jason new webinar 8:13-8:47]. (Q7)
11. Product: demonstrated throughout, every deliverable shown in action, with the client / not-client line [Jason new
    webinar 9:08-9:58].
12. No offer stack on the video: it sits behind the link, "pretty low-key" [Jason new webinar 9:58-10:27].
13. Scarcity is built into the offer (his "Richard Mille style": a fixed number, a release rate, reachable only
    through this path) and kept; never "buy before the end of this video" [Jason new webinar 10:25-11:46].
14. The flywheel: a fixed supply gives a reliable number and a content budget (25 x $2,000 = $50,000 a month); grow
    demand, keep supply, double the price [Jason new webinar 11:46-12:21].

**The funnel**
15. The core video: unlisted, one to three hours, "better to be longer than shorter", calls to action throughout. It
    explains how you work with clients and the resources you built: how to make them yourself, or get them by working
    with you. "It's better to show more than to show less"; edit it down later [Jason new webinar 12:22-13:11].
16. Every other video is an on-ramp that ends by pointing to the core video [Jason new webinar 13:11-13:36].
17. Work backwards: which mindsets make someone ready for the core video, and what content is useful alone but moves
    them toward it [Jason new webinar 13:39-13:59].
18. One slide, one video: "if this one slide could be unpacked for 10 minutes, what would I say?", from more than one
    perspective (the 50,000-ft view, or the weeds) [Jason new webinar 14:01-14:51]. Feeders can feed the feeders
    [Jason new webinar 14:54-15:05].
19. About three videos before most buy, and even 100 would be worth it; old videos keep selling (200-500 views, 1-3
    sales a month): "a fine wine" [Jason new webinar 16:19-16:55].

**Teaching that sells** (his challenges, old habit to new)
20. Inform to transform: "least info needed to change behavior"; value is whether "a person can no longer operate in a
    limited capacity"; often teach less [Jason new way 2:44-3:52].
21. Content to context: "Context says, 'Move away from this and move toward that.'" [Jason new way 3:55-4:34].
22. Objective best to subjective best: teach the option with the lower compliance threshold (often the third best)
    for the market's current consciousness; social media lets you test it [Jason new way 4:34-6:17].
23. Quantity to clarity: "a few concepts repeated in a lot of different ways" [Jason new way 6:17-6:54].
24. Singular to modal: every point as why, what, how and what if [Jason new way 6:54-8:12].
25. Logic to wisdom: logic plus emotion; heart alone gets "a lot of praise, but no buyers", head alone changes no
    behaviour [Jason new way 8:33-9:43].
26. Instruction plus commitment: ask for it before and after teaching; "It's not presentation, it's conversation"
    [Jason new way 9:43-10:41].
27. Fundamentals plus novelty: "If you want a new idea, read an old book", then right-size it with your own touch;
    what lasted 500 years will last, what appeared in the last 5 likely will not [Jason new way 10:41-11:50].
28. Theory to application: give "operations to perform" that a third-party auditor could check [Jason new way
    11:50-12:29].
29. Teach as you sell, in the open: the best do-it-yourself lesson plus "if at any point in time you want help, we
    have resources ... and here's what they look like"; then remind often, because it is "selling for service" [Jason
    new way 12:29-13:28].

**A contrarian video**
30. Open in about 70 s with the nine beats below [Jason AI 0:00-1:09].
31. Build it as verdict / fix pairs: each part opens on a spoken verdict and turns to "instead" inside the part [Jason
    AI 1:03-30:31].
32. Prove each pair with your own operation's numbers and one outside source [Jason AI 1:10, 6:17, 12:34, 27:24].
33. Keep it moving: open loops, provocation announced before it lands, a guess asked before a number, a signpost for
    the last part, self-mockery, one plain analogy per point [Jason AI 2:25, 5:45, 15:30, 23:06].
34. Never "X is dead": "Nothing is dead or alive"; a tactic's value depends on the market's context [Jason AI
    2:48-2:53].
35. Keep the anti-pitch you promise in the hook [Jason AI 0:36].

**How he sells and shoots his own videos**
36. Credentials in the first minute: $5K an hour plus one client number [Jason new webinar 0:00-0:30; Jason new way
    0:18-0:39; Jason AI 0:39-0:55].
37. One mid-roll ad ("Hey, real quick"), recorded in another set, "information is in the bio", after a section's
    payoff, about halfway [Jason new webinar 8:47; Jason new way 8:12; Jason AI 14:27].
38. No subscribe ask, link pill or end card: he ends on a next step, a commitment or "Thoughts?" [Jason new webinar
    15:48-17:18; Jason new way 13:37; Jason AI 31:34].
39. Mostly him on camera (about 75-92%), reframed or cut every 5-20 s, with one printed or handwritten visual; text
    overlays only on the hook's numbers [Jason new webinar 0:59; Jason new way 0:04-0:39; Jason AI 0:29]. Details in
    `playbooks/slides.md`.

**AI on the scripts**
40. AI as a maker is weak (email copy about C+, under 2% of his reels AI-scripted); as a miner it wins (summaries
    about A-) [Jason AI 14:49-17:37]. (Q10)
41. Micro, not macro: one section per prompt, a person checking every step; "If it's losing the context, you are
    asking it to do too much" [Jason AI 9:51, 13:32-13:47, 14:21].
42. AI formalises a process that exists; it never invents one. Mostly it is a placeholder: "plug out the AI, plug in
    the human" [Jason AI 21:40-21:54, 31:12-31:32].

## How to do it

**Build the funnel** [Jason new webinar 12:22-15:05]
1. Make the core video: unlisted, one to three hours, calls to action throughout.
2. Put the calls to action on it: link in the description, pinned comment, DM.
3. End every other video with a line to the core video.
4. Work backwards to the mindsets and stand-alone topics that lead there.
5. Mine ideas: each webinar slide or key point becomes a 10-minute video, from more than one angle.
6. Add feeders for the feeders. Grow reach, keep supply fixed, raise the price.

**Study another channel** [Jason new webinar 15:15-15:45]: what video do they link at the end? Listed or unlisted? How
did the previous video set it up? How do they sell: pinned comment, link in the description, DMs, phone numbers, an
opt-in with a sales follow-up?

**The YouTube two agendas** [Jason new webinar 5:21]: "I'm going to break it down for you exactly how we're successful
with [thing] and how we're helping so many other people get [result] and I'm going to show you how we can work
together in different ways throughout the video."

**The demonstration line** [Jason new webinar 9:24]: "This is the resource that we created that helps our clients do
[this thing]. If you're one of our clients, this is how we apply it for you. If you aren't our client, this is how you
can create your own version of this resource."

**The on-ramp ending** [Jason new webinar 13:25-13:32]: "And if you need more help in [this area], then check out this
video." / "If you want to see how we do this for clients, check out this video."

**One teaching point, four perspectives** [Jason new way 7:34-7:55]: "Here's why this is important. Here's what's
involved. Here's how to use it. And here's what could happen if it goes well for you. And here's what you can adjust if
it doesn't go well for you."

**The commitment loop** [Jason new way 9:51-10:22]: before: "I'm going to show you [this]. If I show it to you and it
resonates with you, then commit to me that you [will] make a go of it ... Is that fair?" After: "Now I've taught you
this. Can you see how this can help you in [this way]? And will you commit to me to actually making this show up in
your life?"

**Edit checks** [Jason new way 6:41, 9:35-9:43]: every day, "How can I communicate the same thing in a slightly
different way?"; too logical, add emotion; too emotional, back it with logic.

**The contrarian hook, beat by beat** [Jason AI 0:00-1:00]
1. A confession: you could profit from [the hype] ("AI is a great shovel to sell").
2. The problem as a picture (too many shovels, not enough gold).
3. A betrayal the viewer already felt ([the last thing that overpromised]).
4. The pain ("promising you [X] while making you [the opposite]").
5. The turn: "But my friend, all is not lost."
6. The anti-pitch: "without selling you [a course, an app, an agent] at the end of this video".
7. Two credential numbers.
8. The bridge: "as good as [X] is, it can't replace [part 1]. Which is where we're going to start."
9. The first verdict, then the outside proof on screen.
Each part after it: "[X] is [verdict]." ... "Instead, [fix]." [Jason AI 1:03, 3:50].

**Research a video topic** [Jason AI 17:29-19:13]: have AI summarise about 50 videos on it; pull the key ideas; write
your bets first ("I bet this will be the insight"); watch a couple; check the bets.

**Find angles** [Jason AI 4:07-4:46]: "Here's my idea. Give me 50 different examples of how this idea can show up."
Expect 45 bad, 4 promising, 1 good; ask for 50 more around those; repeat.

**Count the clips** [Jason AI 20:22-20:44]: run each long recording's transcript through AI for the number of clips it
holds (40-45 from one car session) and hold the editors to it.

## Examples

- Richard Mille offer: "I will never sell more than 25" at $2,000, $50,000 a month, a fixed content budget [Jason new
  webinar 11:46-12:03].
- The pop quiz ("Keanu Reeves, pop quiz, hot shot") still works live in 2026, never on YouTube [Jason new webinar
  4:17-4:43].
- Five ways ranked best to worst: the third has the lower compliance threshold, so people adopt it [Jason new way
  4:44-5:42]; "This is why A+ students work for C- bosses" [Jason new way 6:05].
- The person who cannot focus, taught one thing, now focused: value is the changed behaviour [Jason new way 3:23].
- AI hook proof: the Harvard Business Review "trendslop" article on screen; Israetel's AI-written book at 1.9 on
  Amazon [Jason AI 1:10, 6:17].
- His exponential move: a guarantee so strong ("we'll buy your business and pay you $25,000 on top") that "the funnel
  can still be dog [ __ ]" [Jason AI 24:15-24:46].
- Analogies, one per point: shovels and gold, the bucket brigade, ten rabbits, a hammer not a screwdriver [Jason AI
  0:00, 9:24, 23:44, 31:01].

## Mistakes he calls out

- Porting the live webinar as is: a hard pitch, a stack, end-of-webinar bonus scarcity on a static video [Jason new
  webinar 2:06-2:27, 10:25].
- Asking for comments as an opener: "You're going to look like an idiot" [Jason new webinar 4:25].
- Scarcity you do not keep [Jason new webinar 11:42].
- "The old guru way": informing, teaching the objective best, too many concepts, one modality, all logic or all heart,
  theory, hiding the sale [Jason new way 1:52-13:28].
- AI slop "that looks good, but isn't good": his clients got worse at webinars [Jason AI 13:58-14:10]; context rot in
  long chats [Jason AI 7:42]; agents built on agents [Jason AI 9:14, 25:51].
- Chasing increments: "If you try to chase 10 rabbits at once, you go hungry" [Jason AI 23:29-23:44].
