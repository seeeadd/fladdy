# Scarcity playbook

Read this when: adding scarcity or urgency, deadlines, limited spots (the close, a fast-action bonus, a webinar-only special, a launch window, an offer sold from YouTube).
Sources: `sources/fladlien/notes/full-methodology.md` L16919-18089 (Webinar Scarcity Masterclass), plus the bonus section's scarcity parts L15085-15119, L15571-15591, L15939-15955; `jason-fladlien-videos/02 The New Webinar (VtXp0UwwK3Y)/teardown.md` (scarcity, Richard Mille, 10:25-12:21, 16:47). Built 2026-10-29. The source is the master: re-check a quote there.

## In 5 lines
- Only real scarcity: a limit you will enforce, a reason why, and proof.
- Fit the type to the offer: service = capacity, digital = time or bonuses, event = seats.
- Say exactly what changes, and when; repeat it; then enforce it every time.
- Live webinar: the end-of-webinar bonus turns the room's energy into sales. YouTube: no event, so make the offer itself scarce (fixed supply, a release rate).
- Two or three scarcity elements, never a pile.

## The rules

**The principle**
1. "Scarcity is the greatest motivator of all time": people move more to avoid a loss than to get a gain. [doc L16925-16931]
2. Five types: the price goes up; bonuses disappear (the offer stays); the offer closes (strongest, needs the strongest reason); the window passes ("the world will be different"); a combination. [doc L16933-16973]

**The three pillars**
3. A reason why. Four that work: reward fast action takers (an identity play); keep it exclusive ("Otherwise the offer isn't special"); external constraints, "It's Out of My Hands" (room size, partner rules, capacity), so you are not the bad guy; beta or first movers. Any reason raises compliance (the copy machine study). [doc L16979-17027]
4. Believability: visual proof (sales screenshots, live timers, spot counters); the exact consequence ("The price WILL go to $X on this date"); history ("Last time we sold out in 6 minutes"); how it works ("We can only take 75 clients, here's why..."). [doc L17029-17077]
5. Words: limited, short time, while it lasts, first mover advantage, miss out, before it's gone, exclusive, deadline, early bird, first come first served, now, rare. Sprinkle them, factual and tied to the reason, never overused. [doc L17079-17119]

**Tactics**
6. The catch: build the offer, then "There is a catch though...": the catch is the deadline, so it lands as relief. [doc L17123-17155]
7. The bad news: "Not everyone will get this pricing", "reserved for fast action takers only", naming real fast action takers. You are the good guy. [doc L17157-17191]
8. Fast action bonus stack: extras only for the first [25] or the first hour, with a clear cutoff. It adds value without discounting. [doc L17193-17225]
9. Pre-release price: launch date, retail price, pre-launch price, "Only time ever offered at this price!", which kills hope of a later discount. [doc L17227-17259]
10. The replay edit: tell live viewers an offer (a low downsell, a special) will be edited out of the replay. [doc L17261-17303, L17755-17781]
11. The delete point: lifetime access instead of monthly, today only, with a visible "END OF DELETE POINT" marker. [doc L17305-17337]
12. Capacity: the real limit, the numbers, past sell-outs, "This isn't a sales tactic, it's reality". [doc L17339-17381]
13. Qualification: limited AND you must qualify ("members in good standing"). It feels exclusive, not pushy, and improves customer quality. [doc L17383-17417, L17783-17807]
14. Never again: "This is the only time...", why, and what changes. You must honour it. [doc L17419-17453]
15. Countdown uncertainty: "We're open until [date] OR until we hit [X number], whichever comes first." [doc L17735-17753]
16. Layers catch different people: a quantity limit, a price rise, a fast-action bonus, an early close. [doc L17713-17733]
17. Scarcity can frame the whole webinar, not only the close: a beta launch from minute one, a contractual obligation ("My hands are tied on this"), a pre-release price. [doc L17455-17515]

**How it works on the mind**
18. Loss aversion ("Miss out on $X in bonuses" beats "Get $X in bonuses"); scarce reads as valuable, so "sold out" is social proof; reactance (people want what they can't have, and turn angry if the threat was fake); scarcity raises value, which justifies the scarcity. [doc L17517-17581]
19. It changes the question from "Should I buy this?" to "Can I afford to lose this opportunity?", and helps people make the decision they already want to make. [doc L17987-18001]

**Ethics**
20. Never fake it: limits you don't enforce, "Only 3 left!" that resets, deadlines that extend, capacity that doesn't exist. [doc L17841-17863]
21. No natural scarcity? Create it through process (clients a month, manual onboarding), time (launch periods, seasons, real betas), bonuses (the offer stays, bonuses expire), or an introductory price that really goes up. [doc L17865-17903]
22. Best to worst: real operational limits; time with a clear reason; bonus-based; arbitrary limits you don't enforce are dangerous. [doc L18017-18027]
23. Be more transparent than you think you need to be. [doc L17905-17925]
24. Establish value first: scarcity on a low-value offer doesn't work. [doc L17951]
25. The test: genuine, justified, provable, valuable, respectful. [doc L18043-18057]

**Live webinar vs YouTube (The New Webinar)**
26. Live, the end-of-webinar bonus is "how you churn the energy into profit". On YouTube "it's hard to do any scarcity ... cuz it's a static video. It's not an organic dynamic event." [Jason new webinar 10:25-11:46]
27. So design the offer to be scarce, a "Richard Mille style of offer": "We only have this many, we only release it at this rate, and if you get it, you're in. If not, you'll have to wait till the next round." [Jason new webinar 10:50-11:08]
28. The strongest version: an offer found only through the video and the YouTube webinar, for people who qualify, with "an extreme limit on the availability". "And then, you obviously stick to that." [Jason new webinar 11:24-11:42]
29. A fixed supply gives a reliable number (25 packages a month at $2,000 is $50,000, "I will never sell more than 25") and a content budget. Grow reach, keep supply fixed, raise the price. Selling out becomes its own social proof. [Jason new webinar 11:46-12:21, 16:47]

## How to do it

**Five questions first** [doc L17627-17677]
1. Which type fits? Event or physical: quantity. Digital: time or bonus expiry. Service: capacity. High-ticket: qualification.
2. What is the honest reason? None: don't use that type. Weak: maybe not ready.
3. How will you prove it? Screenshots, numbers, history, live counters.
4. What exactly happens after? The new price, closed for good or reopening, which bonuses go.
5. Can you enforce it (close it, raise the price, turn people away)? Any "no": rethink.

**Checklist** [doc L17929-17981]
- Before: type, reason, the exact change, proof, wording and timing, whole-webinar frame or close only, timers and cut-offs, what happens to those who miss it.
- During: value first, the reason, the proof, the consequence, a clear call to action, the deadline repeated, scarcity questions answered directly.
- After: enforce it (most important); reminders and countdown language; remaining spots or time in emails; close when you said; reopen only if built in from the start; use the enforcement as proof next time.

**Templates** [doc L17681-17709, L17153-17155]
- Fast action: "I want to reward the people who take action quickly, the fast action takers like [names]. That's why [scarcity]. After [deadline], [consequence]."
- Exclusivity: "For this to remain special and exclusive, we can only [limitation]. If we opened it to everyone, [negative outcome]. That's why [scarcity]."
- External: "Here's the reality: [external factor] means we can only [limitation]. This isn't a marketing tactic: it's [reason]. That's why [scarcity]."
- Beta: "We're looking for [X] people to [be founding members]. This is the only time we'll [offer at this level] because [reason]. After [deadline], [change]."
- History: "Last time we did this, [outcome]. We had [X people] try to get in, but only [Y] could. This time we have [Z spots], and based on [data], I expect [consequence]."
- Catch: "Just one $[price] investment gets you EVERYTHING... There is a catch though... The offer expires shortly."

## Mistakes he calls out
- Scarcity with no reason: it trips BS detectors. [doc L17585-17591]
- Unbelievable limits: "Only 1000 spots!" on a digital product. [doc L17593-17599]
- Not enforcing: reoffering after the deadline "Destroys all future credibility". [doc L17601-17607]
- Too much at once ("Only 3 spots left! Price increases in 10 minutes!"): 2-3 elements at most. [doc L17609-17617]
- A vague deadline: give dates, times, timers, counters. [doc L17619-17625]
- On YouTube, "buy before the end of this video" (teardown candidate rule, not yet adopted). [Jason new webinar 10:25-11:46]

## After Harvest

**From the doc** (in the bonus section, for the Black Friday offer)
- Five layers, "all REAL, all logical": time ("Black Friday only happens once a year"), capacity ("We can only take 10 clients for Black Friday this year"), bonuses only on this call, a fast-action bonus ending in 48 hours, the webinar-only email writing. [doc L15939-15955]
- A scarcity line on every bonus: not sold elsewhere, "never been public before and never will be", "First 100 people only", "Only available during this webinar". [doc L15577-15591]

**Our reading** (ours)
- We sell a service, so the decision tree and the Richard Mille offer both point to capacity: a real number of event builds a month, or a real launch window.
- Fladdy's YouTube videos carry no end-of-video deadline; any limit they show is that real capacity.
- A client's 3-day event is live, so its Day 3 close can use the live tools (webinar-only special, fast-action bonus, a cart close), each enforced.
