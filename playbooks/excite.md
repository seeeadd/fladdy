# Excite playbook

Read this when: making an offer, a result or a promise feel exciting; turning features into outcomes; using facts, stats and testimonials; the person vs process close; the intro's promise.
Sources: `sources/fladlien/notes/full-methodology.md` L3321-4010 (Jason Fladlien, "Step 4: Excite"). Built 2026-10-29. The source is the master: re-check a quote there.

## In 5 lines
1. Never stop at the feature: say what it is, what it does, and how it changes their life.
2. Use at least one excitement technique heavily (exploit, results, bling, easy, aspiration, story, quick, timing), then sprinkle the others.
3. Put it in the intro, push to the edge of hype, pull back a little, and keep it going to the end.
4. Prove it with a stack of many, varied testimonials: extraordinary claims require extraordinary proof.
5. Mix push and pull, pain and gain, so it never reads as too good to be true.

## The rules

**What excitement is**
1. Excitement is promises, benefits, outcomes and hope; tease is one specific form of it. Tap the universals that excite almost anyone. [doc L3325-3339]
2. Feature = what it is. Benefit = what it does. Outcome = how it changes your life. [doc L3343-3351]
3. On his $3,125-an-hour consults, nine times out of ten the fix is turning a feature into a benefit, then an outcome. It is discipline, not intelligence. [doc L3371-3379, L3965]
4. Drip with benefits, outcomes and pain; pain is "just a benefit in different clothing". [doc L3407-3411]
5. Stack and dimensionalize: say the same benefit in many different turns of phrase (the summary says 7). [doc L3493-3495, L4007]

**The techniques** [doc L3413-3431]
6. Exploit: "It shouldn't be possible under ordinary conditions." A power reversal, taking advantage of something, a real legitimate secret, a "glitch in the matrix". Rare, so he hunts for it all the time. [doc L3433-3459]
7. Name the exploit out loud and draw the implication: "Amazon is, before anything else, a search engine... And algorithms can be manipulated", so the only question left is whether it is ethical. Lean into self-interest rather than ignore it. [doc L3501-3515]
8. Let them off the hook: name what does little or nothing for the result, and turn a missing thing into a benefit. The exciting implication: "I don't have to get better to get a better result." [doc L3477, L3523-3533]
9. Results: "Everywhere you look you see someone winning!" Quantity (and quality), visual, claim validation: result after result on successive slides. [doc L3535-3559]
10. Bling: objects and destinations (home, car, toys, travel). The easiest way to show success, and the most superficial. [doc L3605-3621]
11. Easy: "People would rather put in almost no effort to get an okay result than a lot of effort to get a big result." Little effort, no experience required, something beats nothing. Break the process into simple steps; show it working (a live phone demo). [doc L3623-3661]
12. Aspiration: purpose, the higher self, motivation beyond money. [doc L3663-3687]
13. Story: easy to take in, entertaining, a "happily ever after" ending; a before/after arc. [doc L3689-3709]
14. Quick: "Smaller results sooner are better than bigger results later." Instant gratification, less risk, a reason or excuse to act. [doc L3711-3731]
15. Timing (bonus): "Your biggest advantage is you just happen to be at the right place at the right time." The ultimate exploit, with urgency built in: the window is open now. [doc L3733-3757]
16. Also excitement tools: analogy, before we dive in, it gets better, tease the mechanism, giveaway (see `curiosity.md`). [doc L3431]

**Facts, stats and testimonials**
17. Stat technique: a wild claim, a stat that backs it, then a tie-down (a rhetorical question or conclusion they agree with). [doc L3759-3771]
18. Stack testimonials: not 2-3 but 8, 10, 15+, so "everyone is winning". [doc L3773-3779, L3979]
19. Use a lot of them in the introduction: they set the stage of what's at stake. Before an important point, qualify it with results first. [doc L3799-3821]
20. Find every testimonial the client has; most people sit on their best ones. One big name can be enough for corporate buyers. [doc L3561-3571, L3781-3783]
21. Character testimonials (who someone is) count as well as results; he calls them "supposedly fair game" when a name cannot be used directly. [doc L3573-3583]
22. Reuse testimonials across presentations of the same product. [doc L3813-3815]
23. Make them diverse in age, background, experience and starting income: that sets up person vs process. [doc L3785-3795]

**Person vs process close** (one of his favourites)
24. A person needs skill and experience; a process works for anyone. Show diverse proof, then tie down: if it is the person, they are the variable; if it is the process, they are not. [doc L3823-3863]
25. You must prove it: "extraordinary claims require extraordinary proof." [doc L3865-3867]

**Implementation**
26. Make sure excitement is in the intro. [doc L3873]
27. Push to the edge of hype, then pull back a little. Go harder than feels comfortable, then calibrate. [doc L3875-3879]
28. Use at least one technique thoroughly; then try more and garnish with the rest until it is second nature. [doc L3881-3887]
29. Excitement continues throughout, not only in the intro. [doc L3891-3893]
30. Push-pull enhances excitement "without becoming manic": all praise and no flaws is too good to be true. [doc L3895-3899]
31. Carrot and stick: the pain and the gain, the benefit and the drawback, the limitation and the miracle, the aspiration. [doc L3905-3915]

## How to do it
1. List every feature. Rewrite each as three lines: "Here's what it is / here's what it does / here's how it makes your life better" (or "here's why tomorrow's gonna be better than today"). [doc L3381-3405]
   Template: "[Feature]. So you can [benefit]. Which means [outcome: how their life is different]."
2. Hunt for an exploit or a timing window first; then choose a main technique and plan its slides (results, easy, quick, story, aspiration, bling). [doc L3439, L3735-3741, L3881-3887]
3. Exploit setup: "Here's all the stuff that would be wonderful if it could happen. It's never happened before. It's unlikely... But if it could happen, do you see how we both could win?" A powerful exploit then gets a responsibility frame: "promise me you will always and only use this to [the good use]... It is that powerful." [doc L3461-3467, L3489-3491]
4. Stat: "[Wild claim]." / "[Specific stat that backs it]." / "[Rhetorical question or conclusion they agree with]." [doc L3763-3767]
5. Testimonials in the intro: "What I'm about to share with you is so important, I have to properly qualify it first. And how do I do that? With results." Then 8-15+ varied results, each "[Name]: [age or starting point]. [Time] later, [result with a number]." [doc L3799-3811]
6. Person vs process: "When you break the code, it isn't the person anymore, it's the process. If it's a person, it would require skill and ability and intelligence and experience. But if it's a process, then anybody of any age... could do it... So person or process?" [results] "It's not the person, it's the process. And that's exciting. If you just follow the process, you can achieve the [result]." [doc L3829-3861]
7. Read it back: count features against benefits and outcomes; add a drawback or a pain where it is all praise; say the main benefit several ways.

- Webinar: feature the webinar, benefit more money, outcome "you never have to look at a price tag again". [doc L3361-3369]
- Mission statement: "I want the fact that you just know me to be your completely unfair advantage..." [doc L3469-3473]
- Launchpad: many turns of phrase for "make money", "Launchpad don't care" how you use it, then "if you wanted to set the world on fire just to watch it burn, you could. So please don't." [doc L3479-3497]
- Kick the giant: "If it doesn't move, we go to the next product. If it stirs, we kick it again. But if it awakens, we win. We win big." Packaging, uniqueness and quality do little, so V1 ships in a white box, "we're ecological". [doc L3517-3533]
- Gender Intelligence: an unused Tony Blair testimonial ("The best of the bunch is Barbara Annis"), found by going through every testimonial she had. [doc L3561-3571]
- Rick Barker could not use Taylor Swift as a testimonial; Jason would play the clip of her thanking her manager on stage and ask "you tell me your thoughts": a character testimonial. [doc L3573-3583]
- OMG Project Assimilation: Daryl, 67, six figures in debt, now $1,500 a day; David and Tess, no English, $17,000 in 90 days; Michael, 19, $42,000; Josh, from a $20,000 a year job to $10,000 a month in four months; six more, then the tie-down. [doc L3591-3603, L3835-3849]
- Wholesale Formula: one picture of customers aged 17 to 77; "as an individual, you are unique... But in Amazon, you're just another cog in the machine... It's not you, it's the process." [doc L3851-3861]
- Each technique runs over several slides: 8 testimonial slides (ASM5), 9 of doing it all from a phone (Mobile Pro, easy), 5 of cars, homes, travel (Copy Eclass, bling), an 8-slide story (Genius Webinars), 6 of "now is the time" (Consumables System, timing). ## Mistakes he calls out
- "Too many features. Not enough benefits. And oh, so few outcomes." Even his own Launchpad copy. [doc L3407-3409, L3497]
- Being timid: not pushing to the edge of hype. [doc L3875]
- Excitement only in the intro. [doc L3893]
- All praise, no flaws: "people don't believe you because it's too good to be true." [doc L3899]
- Fake secrets: an exploit is a real one, "not the bullshit stuff people say is a secret". [doc L3449]
- Testimonials left unused, or only 2-3 shown; a big claim without proof. [doc L3569, L3779, L3867]

## After Harvest
From the doc: none. This range has no After Harvest applications.

Our reading (ours, not Jason's):
- Run every After Harvest claim through feature, benefit, outcome: a 3-day live event is the feature; the outcome is the client's life after it, in their words, never ours.
- Results, testimonials and person vs process need real, sourced After Harvest client results; unknown stays out. On slides a stack is many quick slides, each built as it is said (`rules/slide-method.md`).
- Timing may fit the January launch pitch [ruling 2026-10-28], only with a real reason the window is now.
