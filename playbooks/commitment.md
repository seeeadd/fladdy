# Commitment playbook

Read this when: getting small commitments and yeses from the audience, chat engagement, building to the pitch.
Sources: `sources/fladlien/notes/full-methodology.md` L7767-8992 ("Jason Fladlien's Webinar Commitment Framework",
study notes on his step 8 webinar). Built 2026-10-29. The source is the master: re-check a quote there.

## In 5 lines
1. Never jump from teaching to the offer: build a bridge of commitments between them [doc L8449-8461].
2. Ask for small yeses everywhere: in the intro, before a point, to tie a point down, inside the mechanism [doc L7821].
3. Climb from small to big: listen, makes sense, can see it helping, would do it, would pay for it [doc L8637-8647].
4. Ask, don't claim: let them judge the value ("How valuable would that be?") [doc L8687-8697].
5. Turn each objection into a "right?" they agree with before they raise it [doc L8655-8665].

## The rules

**What a commitment is**
1. Hundreds of small agreements, said or only thought, through the whole presentation; never one big yes at the
   end [doc L7799-7805].
2. Each yes keeps them consistent with the last, so buying feels like the next step, not a leap (Cialdini's
   commitment and consistency) [doc L7809-7817, L8917-8925].
3. Jason names four places (see Lines worth keeping) [doc L7821-7823]; the notes sort them as intro, before
   content, throughout content, between content and offer, and advanced [doc L7831-7841].
4. The ladder, always smaller to bigger: will you listen, does this make sense, can you see how this would help,
   would you do this if you could, would you pay for this [doc L8635-8651].

**In the intro (first 5-10 minutes)**
5. What's your why: have them name a why that is specific ("$500 additional in my bank account tomorrow", not "I
   want to make money"), sensory (see, hear, feel, taste, smell) and involves others (the kids' vacation)
   [doc L7845-7887].
6. On the surface: say their doubt out loud, challenge it, then ask a small favour: listen unbiased
   [doc L7889-7917].
7. Maybe: ask them to consider, not yet to buy. "Just" shrinks the ask; "make your own conclusions" removes the
   pressure [doc L7919-7949, L8803-8811].
8. With that said: "Suppose there was a way..." plus a few benefit bullets (each a small yes), then one question
   that locks it in [doc L7951-7989].
9. My agenda: say openly that you will sell and that the teaching proves the offer is a great deal; end on "Sound
   good?". The offer becomes a reward, not a trick [doc L7991-8015].

**Before the content**
10. The if-then: before teaching, ask if they would try it. Conditional, exclusive ("ONLY myself and my
    customers"), soft ("MAY"), personal ("for YOU"), then "Then WILL you try it?" [doc L8019-8065].
11. What I need from you: an implementation slide. Agreeing that staying stuck would be painful commits them to act
    [doc L8067-8103].
12. Deal?: show the parts to come (Offer, Price, Bonus, Guarantee, Objections) and ask "Deal?". They agree to the
    process, not the purchase [doc L8105-8123].
13. A prize that needs them there ("must be present to win") gets an email, a promise to stay and attention spent
    [doc L8271-8301, L8717-8729].

**Through the content**
14. After each major point: "Does this make sense?" [doc L8853].
15. Proof first, then "Would you like to know exactly how we did it?": a commitment to pay attention
    [doc L8127-8153].
16. The tougher question: an easy yes, then a heavy moral one that makes not acting feel wrong [doc L8155-8191].
17. Three-step ladder: obvious benefit, deeper benefit, final outcome; each yes leads to the next
    [doc L8193-8221].
18. Make them price it: show something useful, ask how valuable it would be, then whether they would pay for it
    [doc L8223-8253, L8685-8697].
19. Objections as commitments: state each as a "right?" they agree with; recap the model, then "do you agree?"
    [doc L8309-8311, L8345-8347, L8653-8665].
20. "However... the facts...": admit the hard truth of doing it alone, then put dollar costs on it ("...if not
    more!"). They commit that they don't want to do it alone, and trust the honesty [doc L8349-8385, L8773-8785].
21. Question, facts, question: a big emotional question, plain facts easy to check, then the commitment question
    [doc L8387-8435, L8677-8683].
22. "With that said" and "However" move the talk on without losing momentum [doc L8667-8675].
23. Pattern interrupt: right after an exciting overview, "HOLD UP! Before we go any further..." and an action
    [doc L8259-8303].

**Between content and offer (the bridge)**
24. Content, commitment, offer: Jason draws an arrow labelled "COMMITMENT" from CONTENT to OFFER [doc L8441-8461].
25. Desire for the category first: make them want any solution like this, ask what would make them believe it
    exists, show the proof, then reveal yours [doc L8499-8543, L8699-8707].
26. Metaphor and two options: an abstract story is easier to agree with. Do it yourself or invest for access? Then
    "Is the investment worth it?" [doc L8463-8497].
27. Others like you: a specific result, "just like you", then "Does that mean you CAN?", the key commitment
    [doc L8545-8581, L8709-8715].
28. Proof, easy, soon: proof across many cases, then easy ("point and click") and near ("by the end of this very
    week"), then "What would it mean to you?" [doc L8583-8631].

**At the offer**
29. By the pitch they have agreed: I'll listen; the problem matters; the solution makes sense; others like me did
    it; I can too; this would be valuable [doc L8875-8891].

## How to do it
1. Plan five phases [doc L8821-8891]: intro (why, surface doubt, maybe, agenda, with that said); before content
   (if-then, implementation ask, prize); through content (a question after every point, proof and objection); the
   bridge (metaphor, value the category, proof it exists, "can you?"); the offer (check rule 29). Goal: "they've
   said 'yes' dozens of times."
2. Count the yeses in a webinar and where each sits; find the gaps in yours. Practise "Can you see...?", "Would you
   agree...?", "Does that make sense?" [doc L8945-8961].

Fill-in templates (his patterns, our brackets):
- Surface: "[It] doesn't seem 'that' [impressive]... Looks are often deceiving. Do me a favor: just listen unbiased.
  Can you do that for me?"
- Maybe: "That's all I'm asking of you: just be open minded. Let me show you [the process]. Then make your own
  conclusions. Cool?"
- With that said: "Suppose there was a way to [goal] that... [benefit] / [benefit] / [benefit]. With that said...
  would you make a valid effort to take advantage of this opportunity?"
- Agenda: "My agenda is to give you the best [60 minutes] of [topic] you've ever heard, to prove to you that my offer
  at the end is an incredible deal. Sound good?"
- If-then: "If I were to show you [a process only we and our customers follow], and you believe it MAY be possible
  for YOU to do it, then WILL you try it?"
- Ladder: "Do you think [obvious benefit]? Do you also think [deeper benefit]? And will that lead to [outcome]?"
- Value: "How valuable would that be? And the real question: would you pay money for it?"
- Objection: "It doesn't take [thing they think they need] to [result] if you approach it correctly, right?"
- Bridge: "Would you rather [do it yourself] or invest for access? ... Is the investment worth it?"

## Examples
- ASM 5: "the single greatest opportunity in the digital age", then a "Facts" slide (anyone can sell private label,
  Amazon ships, paid every 14 days), then commitment questions [doc L8387-8411].
- The NDA: a real "NDA - Please Read Carefully" on screen, then: more people show up? more committed? more sales?
  [doc L8193-8205].
- The contest: a "12 Month Amazon Million Dollar Business" overview, phases ticked, then "HOLD UP!" and a $1,000 draw
  [doc L8259-8275].
- The DIY costs: 90 emails "at the very least $500", a free report $250, a website $100 [doc L8357-8361].
- OMG Project Breakthrough: a secret society that cracks Google's code, then "Well how about someone who constantly
  produces results!" [doc L8499-8515].
- Ramp Engine: a graph of 234,182 orders "for dozens of products in dozens of different niches", then "WHAT WOULD IT
  MEAN TO YOU" [doc L8583-8597].

## Mistakes he calls out
- Hook, teach, pitch with no bridge: the pitch comes "out of nowhere" [doc L8453-8461, L8895-8913].
- One huge yes in place of a ladder; telling them it's valuable instead of asking; waiting for objections
  [doc L7801-7805, L8655, L8687].
- A vague why: "Vague = Forgettable. Specific = Real." [doc L7853, L8755].
- "Will you buy?" when "Will you consider?" is enough [doc L7923-7925].
- Made-up scarcity: the 100 spots worked because it was real and shown [doc L8813-8819].
