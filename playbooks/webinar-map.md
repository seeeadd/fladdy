## In 5 lines
1. Default to Jason's traditional structure: Intro (hook, pain, gain, position), Content (paradigm, mechanisms,
   demonstrations), Transition (recap, tie-down, choices), Offer (core, price, bonus, guarantee, scarcity).
2. Pain, tease, excitement, commitment, positioning and objections each have a slot, but run through the whole webinar.
3. Value first: the price comes after the teaching and 10-15 minutes of value building; bonuses come after the price.
4. Write the theme as one headline before anything else, and hold 80% of the live room until the pitch.
5. On YouTube the structure changes: no hook, a soft sell throughout, the product shown in action, the stack behind
   the link, scarcity built into the offer.

## The rules
**The structure**
1. Jason's traditional webinar has four parts and fifteen elements. It is one of his five structures, "the one that
   covers the most ground" [Jason new webinar 0:49-1:05].
2. Do not think hook, teach, pitch. Think: hook them with commitment, teach while getting commitment, bridge with
   more commitment, pitch as the natural conclusion [doc L8897-8915].
3. Write the theme as one headline that names the majority outcomes; every page, email, slide and the offer then
   satisfies it [Jeremy 20:25-22:47].
4. The presentation (teaching, framing) and the close are two different halves [Jeremy 1:13:55].

**Steps that run throughout**
5. Pain is front and centre from the beginning, "not buried in the middle", used throughout and most in the close
   [doc L1569-1573, L1867-1869].
6. Tease is "the name of the game in the beginning", then continues all the way through [doc L3259-3267].
7. Excitement must be in the intro and continues throughout [doc L3873, L3893].
8. Positioning: take it at the beginning, hold it through the content, extend it in the pitch [doc L4407-4411]. A
   bigger star can be revealed right before the pitch [doc L4373-4377].
9. Commitments come in the intro, before the content, after each point and between content and offer, so the offer
   feels like the next step [doc L8827-8891].
10. "Close in the intro, close in the content, close in the close" [doc L16893].
11. Scarcity can frame the whole webinar from minute one (a beta launch) [doc L17459-17479], but only after value is
    established [doc L17951].

**Timing**
12. The replay's first 30 seconds are make or break: small talk live, then "Let's begin"; the replay is cut to start
    there and the first 100 words are written to hook replay viewers [doc L411-429].
13. The hook that brings them to the webinar need not be the webinar's own hook [doc L441-443].
14. Build 10-15 minutes of value before the price; the whole price build takes 20-30 minutes [doc L13869-13893].
15. Hold 80%+ of the peak live audience until the pitch starts [Jeremy 1:10:58].

**The offer**
16. The offer slide is the most important slide: on a slide, showing everything, stacking value, with a clear call to
    action [doc L11159-11171].
17. Never mention bonuses during the price reveal [doc L13841-13847]. Bonuses get more time than the core: 1 slide a
    module, 2-4 slides a bonus [doc L14599-14605].
18. Be willing to spend hours handling objections: "That's where all the money is" [doc L16623].

**Building it**
19. Build section by section: Jason runs one or two prompts per section, about 47 in all, and a person checks each
    [Jason AI 10:25-13:47].

**Where the sources order the steps differently**
1. **Tease and commitment.** The doc teaches tease before excite and makes commitment its own step (it numbers Excite
   4, Commitment 8, Offer 10, Price 11, Bonuses 12) [doc L3319, L8987, L11151, L12507, L14571]. Jason's sheet has no
   tease step; commitment is the transition's tie-down [Jason new webinar 1:05].
2. **His prompt folder** runs: pop quiz, challenges you face, bone to pick, excitement stack, on this webinar, why
   listen to me, paradigm shift, then criteria #1 and #2, each why / what / how / next [Jason AI frame 10:34]. Excitement
   comes before the blind bullets ("on this webinar") and positioning comes after both.
3. **Mechanisms and demonstrations** are on the sheet and in the folder ("criteria"), but have no section of their
   own in the doc; the doc names no step 7 [doc L8987, L11151].
4. **The transition.** Sheet: recap, tie-down, choices [Jason new webinar 1:09]. Doc: recap, DIY acknowledgment,
   bridge, responsibility offer, empowerment offer, invitation, program introduction, call to action [doc L10151-10207].
   Jeremy: a bridge that asks permission to pitch ("give me ones in the chat") [Jeremy 1:14:54].
5. **The offer.** Sheet: core, price, bonus, guarantee, scarcity [Jason new webinar 1:09]. The doc's price script
   puts the scarcity setup and reason why before the reveal and objections after it [doc L13513-13563]; its
   multi-stage reveal runs price, payment options, bonuses, then scarcity [doc L13883-13891]. Jeremy: item-by-item
   "so you can" stack, a whole-stack slide, price or no price, then objections, risk reversal, Q&A, repitch on a loop,
   then an outro; no bonus or scarcity step [Jeremy 1:17:57-1:23:02].
6. **When the pitch is named.** Pitch up front: say what you sell in the first 60 seconds [doc L103]; or say in the
   intro that you will sell at the end (two agendas, my agenda) [doc L7990-8001; Jason new webinar 4:56].
7. **Length.** Jason's sheet gives the content 40-60 minutes and the transition 3-5 [Jason new webinar frames
   0:59-1:09]; Jeremy runs about one hour of teaching and one hour or more of close [Jeremy 1:13:55].
8. **The YouTube version** [Jason new webinar 2:34-12:45]: the hook becomes the previous video; the pop quiz becomes
   the YouTube two agendas; positioning yourself becomes positioning the vehicle; the hard pitch at the end becomes a
   soft sell throughout with several calls to action; the product is demonstrated throughout, not revealed; the stack
   moves to the page behind the link; scarcity is built into the offer. The core video is unlisted, one to three hours.

## Mistakes he calls out
- Skipping pain, or burying it in the middle [doc L1863-1869].
- Revealing everything in the first five minutes; teasing only in the intro [doc L3179, L3209].
- Over-stating the paradigm: "Today I'm going to show you the paradigm that will make you realize you need my
  product" [doc L5845-5849].
- Mentioning bonuses during the price reveal [doc L13841].
- On YouTube: a pop quiz in the comments ("a social faux pas"), hard pitching and bonus stacking in public [Jason new
  webinar 2:06-2:27, 4:25].
