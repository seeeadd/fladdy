# Funnel numbers playbook

Read this when: planning the funnel around an event or webinar; setting or checking numbers (budget, opt-in, show,
retention, booking, close); diagnosing a funnel that underperforms; scaling one that works.
Sources: `jeremy-haynes-videos/01 The Most Valuable Webinar Training Youll Ever Watch (P1v-AN3So3A)/teardown.md` (all
sections, plus the frame log's numbers on screen: S01-S04, S34-S35, S39, S57-S62), with its `transcript.md` checked for
exact wording [1:49-1:39:23]. Built 2026-10-29. The source is the master: re-check a quote there.

## In 5 lines
1. Model on cash collected, with a test budget you can lose and still come back tomorrow: $5,000-7,500 a webinar.
2. Every step has a benchmark; when a test fails, fix only the one stat that is easiest to double.
3. An opt-in is not a sale: a confirmation page video, teaching emails, six reminder texts, 12-20 videos in their feed.
4. Promote a cold webinar 3 days out at most (the day itself included), a warm one about 5: early opt-ins show less.
5. Scale 10-30% a day or with more webinars a week until the scale ceiling, then hold and find new messaging or a new
   funnel.

## The rules
**Budget and the model**
1. The test budget is what you can "vaporize" and still walk back into the casino the next day: $5,000-7,500 a
   webinar; times four is the month at one a week, about $20,000-35,000 [Jeremy 2:09-2:19, 6:08].
2. Model on cash collected, never revenue [Jeremy 5:18]. Use conservative rates; the model must still make money
   [Jeremy 2:14, 5:35].
3. Cost per lead is made of three stats: CPM, link click-through rate and opt-in rate. The model tracks only cost per
   lead [Jeremy 2:46].
4. Run Tuesday, Wednesday or Thursday: 7 pm EST for B2C, 11 am or 1 pm EST for B2B. One a week is four improvement
   cycles a month; with the budget and time, add a second the same week [Jeremy 6:08-7:14].
5. Build every webinar for cold viewers, even an organic one: cold-built converts warm, warm-built fails cold
   [Jeremy 7:37, 53:40].

**Opt-in page** (20-50% opt-in)
6. Headline, date, opt-in; a VSL with a curiosity-hook thumbnail (or an image, or AI UGC: "Videos are better than
   nothing"); a countdown; two to three scrolls of supporting text; three colours at most. The headline alone should
   let them opt in with confidence [Jeremy 23:19-26:36, 29:21].
7. The opt-in box floats beside the content on desktop and sits fixed on mobile, with buttons back to it [Jeremy
   29:21-29:57].
8. Collect first name, last name, email and phone, validated, with a TCPA box: phone numbers and texts give
   "substantially higher show rates" [Jeremy 23:51-24:24].
9. Opt-in rate moves with traffic quality, messaging, the majority hook and page speed. AI-built pages beat the same
   content in drag-and-drop builders; host fast (Cloudflare, about $200 a month a site, or Vercel) [Jeremy
   27:43-28:47, 29:57-30:30].

**Between opt-in and webinar**
10. Assume "they're not sold yet" [Jeremy 34:22]. His five back-end selling systems: the confirmation page; "hammer
    them" with 12-20 short and long videos in the feed where you found them; setters who reach out to give value, not
    to qualify; value-dense emails; "AI manipulation mastery" [Jeremy 30:45-39:39]. Emails and confirmation pages move
    show rate most [Jeremy 48:40-49:14].
11. Confirmation page, in this order: a headline that acknowledges the action ("You're in. Here's what to do next.");
    one video, urgency (why act now) or research (radical transparency); breakout videos, one per question that leaves
    people colder if unanswered (what sales is tired of explaining, your best buyers' aha moments); testimonials
    [Jeremy 32:16-42:04].
12. After opting in they switch from scanner mode to justification mode: deciding whether to show up at all
    [Jeremy 42:31].
13. Emails teach and remind; texts only remind. Each email teaches part of the headline's promise, the reminder one
    line before the sign-off [Jeremy 43:19-45:13]. About 6 emails, two a day, plus Zoom's own reminders [Jeremy 47:33,
    49:14, 52:00].
14. Never text the join link: phone viewers convert and stay worse. Send them to email or calendar [Jeremy 48:06].
15. A late opt-in gets only the messages from that point on [Jeremy 52:35].
16. Use stable tools when money is at stake: Zoom Webinars, Roezan for texts, GoHighLevel, ActiveCampaign or HubSpot,
    Wistia, dialer.io [Jeremy 24:24, 46:28-48:40, 1:28:02].

**Promotion window**
17. Cold: 3 days at most counting the webinar day, ideally 2 days out; further only with $100K+ budgets. Do not put
    all spend on day 0: it may not spend [Jeremy 49:47-52:00].
18. Warm: about 5 days, no more, or show rate drops; plan every channel (stories, comment CTAs, TikTok, YouTube,
    old videos' description links, emails) [Jeremy 53:40-54:43].

**Ads**
19. 5 angles x 5 variations (five filming places, say) = 25 ads; 5 to 25 is the range. An angle is a different reason
    to convert, not a rewording [Jeremy 54:43-56:26].
20. Video ads of 30-90 seconds with jump cuts and captions bring higher-intent leads and slightly higher show rates;
    images bring cheaper, lower-intent leads [Jeremy 56:58-57:34].
21. Every script is hook, body, CTA, written by AI; 15-30 ads per client, each with its own hook [Jeremy
    58:42-59:48].
22. Ad sets: all ads in one (1-3 take the spend; match the page to the winning angle), one per angle, or the
    Thunderdome (one ad per ad set, 3-second view exclusion). Ad set budgets, not CBO [Jeremy 1:00:55-1:02:54].
23. One or two webinars a week: lifetime budgets that end about 2 hours before. Three a week or automated: daily
    budgets [Jeremy 1:03:38-1:04:11].
24. Optimize for lead or complete registration first: volume back to the pixel, and it exposes weak messaging.
    Schedule or purchase is a delayed firing event, for frequent or automated webinars [Jeremy 1:05:16-1:08:03].

**Webinar, calls, cleanup**
25. Saying the price gives fewer, more qualified calls; leaving it out gives more, less qualified ones [Jeremy 1:19:03].
26. Frame the call as onboarding, where cash is collected and next steps set [Jeremy 1:25:17-1:25:50]. A close rate
    under 35% means a reputation problem (Reddit, Trustpilot, AI models) or salespeople to train or fire [Jeremy 1:24:11].
27. Cleanup by setters, in the four days after the webinar: dial those who showed but did not book, then those who
    opted in but did not show; send the replay; text from real iPhones. It typically doubles ROAS [Jeremy
    1:25:50-1:28:37].
28. The risk timeline: spend starts about 72 hours out, cash starts on webinar day, about 10 days from the first
    dollar to the last [Jeremy 1:26:12-1:26:58, 1:28:37].

**When it fails, and scaling**
29. Most failed tests break even or make a little [Jeremy 1:29:27]. Run a bottleneck analysis: lay the chain against
    the benchmarks, fix the stat easiest to double (CPM: to halve), touch nothing else [Jeremy 1:29:55-1:34:42].
30. A falling opt-in rate can be ad fatigue, not the page: relaunch the ads, align the page to the ad getting the
    spend [Jeremy 1:34:03-1:34:42].
31. Scale daily budgets 10-30% a day, or add webinars a week; with lifetime budgets, start the next webinar higher.
    Automate a scheduled webinar only after scaling (it slims ROAS), never just in time [Jeremy 1:35:42-1:38:46].
32. Small-scale ROAS is not the true ROAS [Jeremy 1:37:32]. The scale ceiling is set by messaging angle and funnel
    type: there, hold the campaign and find new messaging or a new funnel [Jeremy 1:37:08-1:38:46].
33. Use cost-per-result goals, not plain automated bidding; update the model as the stats move [Jeremy 1:39:17].

## How to do it
**The benchmark table** (his bottleneck chain in order [Jeremy 1:30:16-1:31:20], the averages he gives elsewhere,
and his made-up doubles-game example [Jeremy 1:32:11])

| step | target | average / warning | example | stamp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CPM | $30-80 | | $56 | 1:30:48 |
| link click-through rate | 2%+ | | 1%: double it | 1:30:48 |
| opt-in rate | 20-50% | his page 30%+, a client 20%+ | 15%: double it | 26:36-27:10 |
| cost per lead | $5-12 good | $20 conservative, $30 very, up to $40 | | 3:11 |
| show rate to the webinar | 30%+ (to 44% with his confirmation pages) | 20% average; 10-15% something is broken | 27% | 0:29, 3:52 |
| retention to the pitch | 80%+ of peak (masters 95%+) | | 81% | 1:11:50-1:12:59 |
| booking rate (of those at the pitch) | 30%+ | 20% below average; 10-15% well below | 30% | 4:28 |
| booked-call show rate | 70%+ | | 65% | 4:28, 1:23:37 |
| close rate on calls | 35%+ | | 34% | 4:28, 1:23:37 |
| AOV (cash collected) | higher is better | model $3,500 | $4,200 | 5:00, 1:33:35 |
| email open rate | 40-60% | under it: deliverability, even at a ~100% score | | 47:33, 48:40 |
| text open rate | 80-90%+ | | | 47:33 |
| show rate by opt-in day | day 0: up to 40%+ | day 3 or earlier: 10% or less | | 50:56 |

His verdict on the example: fix link click-through and opt-in [Jeremy 1:33:01-1:34:03].

**The model** (blanks to fill; his worked example in brackets) [Jeremy 5:00-5:35; frames 2:09-5:59]
- budget [$5,000] / cost per lead [$20] = registrants [250]
- x show rate [20%] = at the webinar [50]; x booking rate [30%] = calls booked [15]
- x call show rate [70%] = calls taken [11]; x close rate [35%] = sales [about 4]
- x cash per sale [$3,500] = cash [$12,863]; cash / budget = ROAS [2.57]

**The six texts** [Jeremy 45:22-45:55]: at opt-in; the day before (about 24 hours); the morning of; 1 hour before;
10 minutes before; at the start. About 10 minutes in, no-shows get "are you having trouble joining?", answered by a
person or an AI agent.

**The run** (his twelve parts, in order [Jeremy 0:00-1:39:23])
1. Budget, model, day and time. 2. Theme headline (webinar-map.md). 3. Opt-in page. 4. Confirmation page. 5. Emails
and texts. 6. Ads, live about 72 hours out, off 2 hours before. 7. The webinar, 80% held to the pitch. 8. The close
(close.md). 9. Calls. 10. Cleanup. 11. Bottleneck analysis if it failed. 12. Scaling if it worked.

## Examples
- Co-living: an AI agent found 13 major US cities had rolled back zoning laws; an 8-minute "why now" video, first on
  the confirmation page, lifted show rate 19% [Jeremy 35:26-38:10].
- His research video shows his dispute rate, refunds, top and average results [Jeremy 34:22-34:53].
- His first email, "Here's the first lesson - confirmation pages", is all teaching, with the reminder in the last
  sentence [Jeremy 43:07-43:39].
- Audiobook funnel: opt-in 28%, 5% after ad fatigue, 25% once the ads were relaunched [Jeremy 1:34:03].

## Mistakes he calls out
- Optimizing around revenue: "Don't be one of those dummies" [Jeremy 5:00].
- Vibe-coded webinar platforms that crash [Jeremy 46:28-47:00].
- Angles that "barely describe the same thing differently" [Jeremy 55:51].
- Just-in-time webinars: "violently bad" [Jeremy 1:36:24].
- Cleanup by calls alone, or texts from "blue text services" [Jeremy 1:27:32-1:28:03].

## Lines worth keeping
- "Optimize around the actual dollars that hit your payment processor." [Jeremy 5:18]
