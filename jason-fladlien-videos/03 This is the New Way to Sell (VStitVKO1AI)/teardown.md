## In one paragraph

A 14-minute lesson, mostly to camera, for people who sell online. He argues that "funnels, pitches, direct response
style marketing" have lost their edge and that a specific kind of teaching now does the selling [0:04-0:16]. He
opens with four points (a more informed buyer buys better, independence creates dependence, the less you need the
sale the easier it is, and the top 1% of a market holds 50% of its value) [1:15-1:41], then names the shift: "from
selling as teaching to teaching as selling" [1:48]. Teaching becomes the primary way to sell, and launches,
challenges, funnels and webinars run afterwards on the attention it earns [2:09-2:27]. The spine is a list of
"challenges", each an old teaching habit swapped for a new one, which he writes as a ten-row T-chart on a paper
sketchbook while he talks [2:36-13:28]. He breaks once for his $10,000 in-person mastermind [8:12] and ends by asking
the viewer to commit to the process [13:37].

## The frameworks

**The shift: teaching as selling** [1:48]. "We do it by shifting from selling as teaching to teaching as selling"
[1:48]; "instead of selling to teach, we're now teaching to sell" [1:56]. He calls the old habit "the old guru way"
[2:03] and says to "merge the best of both worlds" [2:06]: teaching is "a primary mechanism to sell", and on the
attention earned "we can still do launches, challenges, funnels and we still do webinars" [2:09-2:18]. The webinar
then sells to people who already trust and understand you, when they are ready [2:18-2:27].

**Education has exceeded marketing** [0:39]. "For the first time in history, education in a specific manner, in a
certain context, with the proper structure behind it, has exceeded marketing. The thing has now become more valuable
than the perception of the thing" [0:39-0:54].

**The four points** [1:15]. "At its heart, there are four important points you must understand":
1. "a more informed consumer makes a better purchasing decision" [1:20];
2. "the more you help to create independence, the more you will get dependence" [1:22];
3. "the less you need the sale, the easier it is to get the sale" [1:30];
4. "1% of the market represents 50% of its total value" [1:34]. "The key to this market, the 1%, is empowerment and
   referral. Education is the most effective way to accomplish this" [1:39-1:44]. Later: teaching first reaches "100
   times the market", and "the 1% of the market that spends 49% of its money" [2:29].

**The challenges** [2:36]. "Here's the challenges that are going to get in your way" [2:36]. He introduced them
earlier: "The challenges that you're going to face with this new model ... They're subtle and they can catch you and
they can make you want to default to the old guru way and only that way" [1:52-2:03]. He has seen them "since I
started teaching this in 2007 and still an issue in 2026" [2:41]. Each one is a row on his chart, old habit on the
left, the replacement on the right (see "What is on screen"):

1. **Inform to transform** [2:44-3:52]. People think teaching means "give people information" [2:50]. "What we're
   really doing is creating transformation. And the way more than any that you create transformation is least info
   needed to change behavior" [3:05-3:11]. The measuring stick of value: "if a person can no longer operate in a
   limited capacity" [3:15-3:21] (the example: someone who cannot focus is taught one thing and is now focused
   [3:23-3:34]). Often the help is removing things that do not serve them, so "this is actually possible to teach less
   and get more out of it" [3:36-3:48]. "We don't inform, we transform" [3:52].
2. **Content to context** [3:55-4:34]. "Content just gives insight or information or education. Context says, 'Move
   away from this and move toward that.' Context is more important than content" [4:08-4:22]. "Context makes it
   subjective. This is what I personally need to do specifically with the objective information that I've been
   given" [4:24-4:31].
3. **Objective best to subjective best** [4:34-6:17]. What he tells his consulting clients: "You're teaching the
   objective best strategy here. You need to change it to the subjective best strategy" [4:37-4:43]. The **compliance
   threshold** [5:12]: the objectively best way "has a high compliance threshold ... it's very difficult for people
   to follow it and execute upon it and get a result" [5:12-5:20]; the third best has a lower one, so "people can
   adopt it more, aka change their behavior and get a better result from it" [5:23-5:42]. "It is almost never the
   case where the objective best option in teaching is the thing you should teach them. You have to teach it
   contextually to the market based upon the consciousness that the market is currently in" [5:42-5:55]. Social
   media gives "an unlimited number of chances" to find what the market responds to [5:55-6:05].
4. **Quantity to clarity** [6:17-6:54]. "In the past, we focused on quantity of information, and now we need to focus
   on clarity and insight of information" [6:17-6:26]. "We don't need a lot of different concepts. We need a few
   concepts repeated in a lot of different ways" [6:26-6:32]. The chain: communication leads to insight, insight to
   connection, connection "creates buyers at a rate that is never seen before" [6:45-6:54].
5. **Singular to modal** [6:54-8:12]. Most people "teach in a singular modality" [7:02]: a why-based teacher teaches
   only why, a what-based teacher only what [7:02-7:24]. "Markets are composed of all different models of reality.
   And so, we have to be modal to that market" [7:26-7:31]. He teaches "the why of it, the what of it, the how of it,
   and the what if of it" [7:34-7:38], to hit "the four different major perspectives that every market has" [7:59].
   Combined, people "get deeper understanding" [8:09-8:12].
6. **Logic to wisdom** [8:33-9:43]. People "default to logic. Cut and dry. First, do this, then do that. Take the
   emotion out of it completely" [8:35-8:43]. "What we need to teach is through wisdom. And wisdom is the combination
   of the right amount of logic with the right amount of emotion seasoned on top of it" [8:51-8:58]. Heart alone gets
   "a lot of praise, but no buyers"; head alone can empower but "won't get them to change their behavior cuz all
   behavior has an emotional component attached to it" [9:04-9:15]. Everyone leans one way by default [9:20-9:30].
7. **Instruction to instruction plus commitment** [9:43-10:41]. "Good teaching is instructional ... But, great
   teaching, teaching that sells, is instructional plus gets commitment" [9:43-9:51]. He asks for commitment before
   and after he teaches (the script is under "The process"). "Teaching is a two-way street ... It's not presentation,
   it's conversation" [10:22-10:33].
8. **Fundamentals plus novelty** [10:41-11:50]. "Real good teaching is mostly fundamentals" [10:41]. "If you want a
   new idea, read an old book" [10:46]; then "right-size it to the new world" with "your flavor, your touch, your
   insight, your novelty" [10:52-10:59]. Novelty is a new idea added to an old one, or "an old idea in a new way or a
   new format" [11:01-11:09]. **Antifragility, robustness** [11:19]: what has lasted 500 years will likely last 500
   more; what appeared in the last 5 years likely will not last 5 more [11:19-11:40]. "Fundamentals are not very fun.
   And so, we make them fun through novelty" [11:45-11:48].
9. **Theory to application** [11:50-12:29]. "The other trap we have to escape is the trap of theory" [11:50]. "You
   got to give people operations to perform" [12:06]. **The third-party theory** [12:16]: "if you are teaching in a
   way where a third-party auditor can't look upon your students, your audience, and see if they complied or didn't
   comply to your instructions, then you have a problem" [12:17-12:29].
10. **Teach then sell to teach as we sell** [12:29-13:28]. "We don't teach anymore and then sell, we teach as we
    sell. We don't have to hide the fact that we're selling" [12:29-12:38]. "We design teaching systems that make it
    obvious to the end users": the very best lesson on doing it on your own, and "if at any point in time you want
    help, we have resources that you can purchase and here's what they look like" [12:48-13:03]. Then you can let
    people know "more aggressively and more assertively that we have things for sale" and remind them repeatedly,
    because "it comes as selling for service, not selling to extract money from a market" [13:08-13:26].

**Earn the right to sell** [13:28]. "The more you teach, the more you earn the right to sell, but selling in 2026
through teaching is different than it's ever been, and it has more upside if you do it right" [13:28-13:37].

## The process, in order

**The model** [2:06-2:36]
1. Teach, as the primary mechanism to sell.
2. On the attention earned, "if we wish to at any point in time", run launches, challenges, funnels, webinars.
3. The webinar sells to people who already trust and understand you, "when you're ready to have it solved".
4. "We reach 100 times the market, we reach the 1% of the market that spends 49% of its money and then we own the
   market."

**Teach any point in four perspectives** [7:34-7:55]. His order, said as five lines:
1. "Here's why this is important."
2. "Here's what's involved."
3. "Here's how to use it."
4. "And here's what could happen if it goes well for you."
5. "And here's what you can adjust if it doesn't go well for you."

**The commitment loop** [9:51-10:22]
1. Before teaching: "I'm going to show you this thing. If I show it to you and it resonates with you, then commit to
   me that you are make a go of it and take it out for a walk and see where it takes you. Is that fair?"
2. The audience: "Yes, Jason. Whatever you teach me, I'm committed to doing if it makes sense to me."
3. He teaches.
4. After: "Now I've taught you this. Can you see how this can help you in this way? And will you commit to me to
   actually making this show up in your life?"
5. The audience: "Yes, Jason. I will."

**The logic and emotion check** [9:35-9:43]: when creating content, "If I'm too logical here, let me add some emotion
to it. If I'm too emotional here, let me validate it and back it up with logic."

**The daily repetition** [6:37-6:43]: every day ask, "How can I communicate the same thing in a slightly different
way?"

**The third-party test** [12:06-12:29]: give people operations to perform, so an outside auditor could see whether
each person did them.

**Teaching that sells in the open** [12:48-13:03]: show what doing it on your own looks like, give the very best
lesson on it, and say plainly that resources can be bought for anyone who wants help, and what they look like.

## Where he differs from Jeremy Haynes

Compared with `jeremy-haynes-videos/01 The Most Valuable Webinar Training Youll Ever Watch (P1v-AN3So3A)/teardown.md`
only.
- **Where the sale happens.** Jeremy builds a webinar for cold paid traffic that books sales calls (Jeremy [1:23:33]).
  Jason makes teaching itself the primary way to sell and treats webinars, launches, challenges and funnels as
  optional, later, on earned attention [2:09-2:27].
- **Who to aim at.** Jeremy's majority hook speaks to "the broadest demographic of financially qualified buyers"
  (Jeremy [12:33]). Jason points at the top 1% that holds 50% of a market's value, reached by "empowerment and
  referral" [1:34-1:41].
- **One message, said again.** Jeremy writes one headline theme that every asset satisfies (Jeremy [22:14]); Jason
  wants "a few concepts repeated in a lot of different ways" [6:26]. Close in spirit; Jason's is about daily
  repetition from new angles, Jeremy's about one promise across a funnel.
- **Asking the audience for a yes.** Jeremy's bridge asks permission to make an offer (Jeremy [1:14:54]). Jason asks
  for commitment to apply the lesson, before and after each teaching [9:55-10:22], and ends the video the same way

- **Hiding the sale.** Jeremy separates an hour of teaching from an hour of close (Jeremy [1:13:55]). Jason says "we
